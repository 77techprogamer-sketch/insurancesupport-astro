LIC New Endowment Plan 714 Review 2026: Bonus Rates, Real Returns & Claims

LIC's most flexible endowment term ladder — with a bonus structure that decides your maturity figure rupee by rupee.

The New Endowment Plan is LIC's flagship participating savings-cum-protection contract — the product family behind most "LIC gives around 6–7%" conversations in Indian households. Plan 714 (UIN 512N277V03, the renumbered continuation of Table 914 and, before it, 814) is unusual in one respect that every competitor review eventually mentions: term flexibility from 12 to 35 years, wide enough to match almost any goal horizon. What the reviews explain less well is the bonus arithmetic that decides your maturity figure. This review walks the current bonus rates (SRB around ₹48 per ₹1,000, FAB around ₹350 per ₹1,000 at long durations from the 2025 valuation), worked maturity numbers, the claim-settlement mechanics, and where Plan 714 sits against Jeevan Labh and market alternatives.

Plan mechanics: entry, term, premium

Entry age runs 8 to 55 years, policy term 12 to 35 years, and the premium-paying term equals the policy term — unlike Jeevan Labh there is no limited-premium variant, so every year of cover costs a premium year. Minimum sum assured is ₹2 lakh (₹25,000 for some micro variants issued under older rules); maximum maturity age is 75. Premiums pay annually, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly, with GST at 4.5% in year one and 2.25% thereafter added to the net premium. The plan participates in LIC's with-profits fund: maturity pays basic sum assured plus accrued simple reversionary bonus plus final additional bonus, and death during the term pays the higher of sum assured, 125% of premiums paid, or the maturity amount, plus accrued bonuses. Suicide within 12 months returns premiums (80% after the first policy year for standard wordings), and non-disclosure of health history remains the live risk on any death claim filed before the Section 45 incontestability period closes.

The bonus arithmetic that decides everything

Two bonus components build the maturity payout. SRB is declared annually per ₹1,000 of sum assured and, once declared, cannot be reversed — recent declarations for the new-endowment bonus group sit near ₹48 per ₹1,000. FAB is a one-time loyalty bonus paid at maturity or death, declared for longer-duration policies — the 2025 valuation's standard FAB for endowment-type groups ran around ₹350 per ₹1,000 for policies around 24 years. A ₹5 lakh, 24-year policy accrues:

Maturity = sum assured + SRB + FAB

Illustrative at current declared rates. SRB/FAB rates are declared yearly and are not contractually guaranteed in advance — only the sum assured is.

The same policy at a 20-year horizon shows why long durations matter more here than anywhere in insurance: FAB eligibility fades on shorter terms, and 4 fewer years of SRB on a ₹5 lakh base costs about ₹96,000 of bonus by itself. Worked 20-year examples online (₹5 lakh SA, roughly ₹4.9 lakh total premiums, maturity around ₹9.25 lakh) reflect exactly this — a little under 2x of premiums, entirely tax-free. The corollary for mid-term buyers: the second decade of a New Endowment policy earns more bonus than the first, which is precisely why surrendering in year seven feels punitive but continuing quietly repairs the arithmetic.

Return reality: what "6–7%" means after costs

On LIC's required 4%/8% gross illustrations, Plan 714 like all participating endowments shows a maturity figure well below the gross assumption — the with-profits spread sits between. In rupee terms on the ₹5 lakh / 24-year example above: total premiums across 24 years at current rates run roughly ₹4.5–₹5.5 lakh for a 30-year-old (age, mode and term move this materially), and the ₹12.5 lakh maturity implies an IRR in the mid-5% range — tax-free, guaranteed-horizon, sovereign-backed. Competitor framing that calls this "7% plus" typically mixes the 8% gross scenario with the payout; the honest comparison set is post-tax PPF (still competitive on the guaranteed side), bank FDs (taxable, so lower effective), and debt funds (taxable with indexation questions). What Plan 714 genuinely offers that those do not: compulsory discipline, an inbuilt death cover at every age, and Section 10(10D) tax-free proceeds within the premium cap. What it cannot offer: equity participation. Over a 24-year horizon, even a conservative 9–10% equity SIP average compounds to materially more than the mid-5% IRR — which is why mixing (term cover + index SIP + PPF) beats any single traditional plan in pure arithmetic, and Plan 714 belongs in the guaranteed-slab of your goal funding, not the growth slab.

Claim mechanics: maturity, death, surrender

Maturity claims need the original bond, discharge vouchers and KYC/bank details — filed at the servicing branch or via the LIC portal where enabled. Death claims need the death certificate, bond, nominee KYC and bank proof; LIC's FY 2024-25 individual death-claim settlement of 98.15% means documented claims largely clear quickly, and the disputes concentrate in undeclared health history (Section 45 gives insurers 3 years to contest on misrepresentation, after which the cover becomes incontestable) and nominee-record mismatches. Surrender (after 3 full years' premiums) pays roughly 30–70% of premiums paid plus bonus surrender value — the classic endowment exit penalty; policy loans after 3 years run up to 80–90% of surrender value at resetting half-yearly rates. What usually goes wrong (and how to pre-empt it)

Three failure patterns account for most New Endowment friction we see. First, premium-mode drift: quarterly and monthly modes carry a mode loading, and a missed debit after the grace period (typically 30 days from due date) lapses the policy — revival requires fresh health declarations after the revival window, which for a 60-year-old holder means medicals that may not clear. If the policy matters, keep one mandate account funded and review the debit date annually. Second, loan-versus-surrender confusion: borrowers surrender when a loan would preserve the contract — the surrender value in the first decade is roughly 30–50% of premiums paid, while the loan facility keeps the policy alive at 80%+ of surrender value against it. Third, maturity-claim filing lag: filing after the maturity date is accepted with the bond and ID, but interest-on-delayed-payment arguments are avoidable by simply submitting discharge papers two weeks before the date. Each of these has a documented remedy path in the claim guides linked above.

Plan 714 vs Jeevan Labh vs Jeevan Umang — the one-paragraph version

Jeevan Labh (736) stops premiums 5–9 years early at a slightly higher annual cost and ends cover at maturity; New Endowment (714) runs premiums the full term and keeps the classic structure; Jeevan Umang pays 8–10% of sum assured annually from age 100 backwards as lifetime income instead of one lump. Buy 714 for a clean single-goal horizon with maximum term flexibility; buy Labh when cash-flow timing matters more; buy Umang when the goal is annuity-like income. Taxation: where every rupee sits (80C, 10(10D), GST)

Three separate provisions touch a New Endowment policy and they interact in ways buyers routinely get wrong. On the way in: premiums qualify for Section 80C deduction — inside the shared ₹1.5 lakh annual ceiling that also holds your EPF, ELSS and home-loan principal. A ₹50,000 annual premium therefore consumes a third of the ceiling; if your employer EPF already fills it, the "tax saving" argument for buying the plan disappears and the decision should rest on returns alone. GST applies to the premium at 4.5% in the first policy year and 2.25% in later years — on a ₹50,000 net premium that is ₹2,250 then ₹1,125, and this GST is not recoverable or deductible. On the way out: maturity proceeds (sum assured plus bonuses) are exempt under Section 10(10D) provided the annual premium for any year does not exceed 10% of the sum assured for policies issued after 1 April 2012 (the older 5% test applies to pre-2012 policies, grandfathered). On a ₹2 lakh sum assured with a ₹25,000+ premium the 10% test is comfortably met; on micro policies with low sum assured it is where claims historically caught people. Death benefit is always fully exempt under 10(10D) regardless of the premium test — protection leg first, savings leg second. Keeping the sum-assured-to-premium ratio healthy at purchase, and holding the bond with KYC current, is the entire tax-risk management plan for this product.

Strengths

  • Term range 12–35 years — the widest ladder in LIC's participating lineup
  • SRB once declared is guaranteed; endowment-group rates near ₹48/1000 with FAB on long terms
  • Tax-free maturity (10(10D)) within premium caps; 80C on premiums
  • 98.15% death-claim settlement; loan facility after 3 years

Weaknesses

  • Premium-paying term = policy term — no limited-premium relief
  • Real IRR mid-5% range, below equity SIPs over the same horizon
  • Surrender in first decade forfeits a large premium share
  • Bonus rates are declared annually — not a contractual promise

Who should buy New Endowment 714

The plan fits the conservative goal-funder: a fixed liability (education instalment, construction completion, daughter's wedding) where the corpus must exist on a date certain and market risk cannot be entertained. It fits salaried buyers wanting automatic discipline with tax-free proceeds and a death cover that runs the whole term. It fits poorly as a retirement primary (the IRR will not beat inflation-adjusted alternatives), as a monthly-income vehicle (it pays once, at the end), or as sole life cover (sum assured multiples and term caps make dedicated term cover cheaper per lakh). If you already hold one — check it against the bonus table above: a policy started in the 2000s at ₹50/1000-class rates is a materially different asset from one bought at today's declarations, and surrendering an old high-bonus-vintage policy to restart a new one is almost always a mistake.

What is the current bonus rate on LIC New Endowment Plan 714?

Recent SRB declarations for the new-endowment bonus group are around ₹48 per ₹1,000 of sum assured per year, and FAB around ₹350 per ₹1,000 at long durations (2025 valuation figures). Rates are declared annually out of LIC's valuation surplus — only the sum assured is guaranteed in advance.

How much return does Plan 714 give?

Worked examples (₹5 lakh SA, 20 years, ~₹4.9 lakh total premiums, ~₹9.25 lakh maturity) imply an IRR in the mid-5% range — tax-free. LIC's own 8% gross illustration reaches the policyholder at roughly 5.3–5.8%; the 4% scenario lands near 2.5%.

Can I stop premiums early on Plan 714?

No — the premium-paying term equals the policy term on New Endowment. If you need premiums to stop 5–9 years before maturity, Jeevan Labh (Plan 736) is the limited-premium variant.

Is the maturity amount tax-free?

Yes, under Section 10(10D) provided the annual premium stays within the statutory cap relative to sum assured; premiums qualify under Section 80C within the ₹1.5 lakh overall limit. GST (4.5% first year, 2.25% thereafter) applies to premiums.
