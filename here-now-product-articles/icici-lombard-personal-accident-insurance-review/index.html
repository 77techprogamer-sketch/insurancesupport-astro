ICICI Lombard Personal Accident Insurance Review 2026: ₹3L–₹25L Cover, Claim Rules

The cheapest large cover in Indian insurance — and the one whose claim depends on definitions most buyers never read.

A personal accident policy is the cheapest large-cover instrument in Indian insurance — and the most mis-sold. ICICI Lombard's Personal Accident Insurance packs accidental death and permanent disability cover from ₹3 lakh to ₹25 lakh, worldwide and 24×7, on entry ages 18–70 renewable to 70, with optional accidental hospitalisation expenses and a daily allowance bolted on. Families buy it in ten minutes as "some extra cover" and then discover at claim time that the accident definition, the intimation clock, and the difference between partial and total disability decide everything. This review runs the actual policy machinery: the permanent disability benefit scale the claim is measured against, the exclusions that kill otherwise-genuine claims, the optional covers that matter, and where ₹3 lakh versus ₹25 lakh of sum insured makes sense.

What counts as an accident — and what silently does not

The policy pays for injury caused solely by external, violent, sudden and visible events — the standard accident definition — while you are doing ordinary things of life. Three boundary cases decide most disputes:

  • Medical events behind the wheel — a heart attack or stroke that causes the crash is an illness claim first; pure personal accident covers do not pay an accidental death where the root cause is disease, even though the crash looks violent. This is the single most contested category in the segment.
  • Intoxication and illegal acts — death or injury while driving under the influence, or during a criminal act, falls in the standard exclusion list across insurers. Road-accident claims also require the FIR/police record; a hit-and-run without documentation stalls the claim regardless of merit.
  • High-risk activities — professional sports, hazardous pursuits and adventure sports sit at the exclusion margin; the fine print varies by insurer, so the activity list should be read before purchase if your life includes trekking, riding or water sports regularly.

What the cover does embrace cleanly: accidents anywhere in the world, at any hour, including road and rail accidents, falls, burns, drowning and acts of terrorism — terrorism cover is explicit in this product, a clause some buyers only recall after a headline event. There is no medical examination at entry; the disclosure burden is light, which is exactly why honest declaration at proposal remains the cheapest claim insurance you will ever file.

The benefit scale: how a permanent disability claim is actually valued

Permanent total disability claims (both hands, both feet, both eyes, or one of each with total and irreversible loss of function) pay the full sum insured. The contested majority are permanent partial disabilities, valued against the standard IRDAI scale:

Loss / disablement% of sum insured typically payable
Both eyes100%
One eye50%
Both hands or both feet100%
One hand or one foot50%
Thumb (any hand)25% (as per standard scale)
Permanent total disablement otherwise100%, as defined in the policy

Two implications follow. First, "disabled" in ordinary language is not "permanently and totally disabled" in policy language — a ₹25 lakh policy can pay ₹12.5 lakh (one eye) or a fraction for a finger, not the headline amount. Second, the scale is why the sum insured choice should start from what a permanent partial loss would actually need: income substitution for a desk worker losing a thumb is a different number from a machine-operator losing a hand. Occupational profile drives the ladder, not the premium card.

The optional covers, and which ones earn their premium

The base accidental death and disability cover can be extended with two practical riders on this product:

  • Accidental hospitalisation expenses — reimbursement of treatment costs arising from an accident, subject to a minimum hospital stay of 24 hours, treatment within a defined window from the accident (commonly within 7 days), and the plan's internal limits. It is a reimbursement route, not cashless — bill, discharge summary and the accident-linked diagnosis must line up, and this is exactly where our cashless claim denied playbook applies for the appeal sequence.
  • Accidental daily hospital allowance — a fixed daily amount for hospitalisation from an accident, typically capped (commonly around 30 days) with one day excluded at the start. It is cash for the non-medical friction of a hospital stay: attendant travel, food, lost income days — modest but real.

₹ sum insured options

Entry cover suits young earners with no dependents; the ladder tops out at ₹25 lakh — anything above that needs life-term cover layered on top, not a bigger accident policy.

Sizing the ladder: ₹3 lakh or ₹25 lakh, and who sits where

The sum insured ladder on this product (₹3, 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 lakh) is not a premium menu — it is a map of who depends on your body for income. The sizing logic that survives contact with real claims runs roughly like this. A single 22-year-old in a desk job with no dependents sits comfortably at the ₹3–5 lakh entry cover: its job is to fund recovery and absorb the income gap of a few months, not to replace a career. A married sole breadwinner in a field, driving or on-site role — the gig, logistics, sales and construction profiles this product exists for — should read ₹10–20 lakh as the working band, because a permanent partial disability (the one-hand, one-eye, one-foot outcomes that pay 50% on the scale) needs to substitute years of reduced earning capacity, not weeks. Homemakers carry a parallel case that families routinely under-buy: no salary slips exist to anchor the decision, yet a permanent injury to a homemaker converts into paid help, childcare and household replacement costs that run for decades — ₹5–10 lakh of cover priced at personal accident rates is the cheapest way to pre-fund that arithmetic. Senior entrants (the policy takes entry up to 70, renewable towards the same ceiling) read the ladder from mobility and fall risk rather than income: the cover funds recovery and attendant costs, since income substitution has usually ended. And above ₹25 lakh — the product's own ceiling — the answer is not a second personal accident policy from another insurer stacked on top; it is term cover, whose death benefit pays for any cause and whose total disability options (where available) add structural protection. Accidental cover is a layer, sized to the accident-specific gap, and every rupee above that gap is better spent on cause-neutral protection.

One premium-shaping detail worth checking at quote time: multi-year tenures (three- and five-year covers alongside the annual option) fix the rate for the whole term and eliminate the renewal lapse — and because entry stops at 70 with renewal following that ceiling, a long tenure bought while healthy is also the cleanest way to remove future medical underwriting surprises from the renewal conversation. Compare the three-year and five-year quotes against the annual one at the same sum insured before paying; the discount, not the brochure, tells you which tenure the insurer is actually keen to sell.

The claim sequence, hour by hour

The clocks are unforgiving: intimation in days, evidence at the scene, the 12-month outer limit. Families that lose valid accident claims almost always lose them on documentation, not on merit.

Two numbers govern the outcome: how fast you intimate, and how well the causation is documented. An accident claim filed after the intimation window, or one where the FIR says "found unconscious" with no external injury evidence, can fail even when the story in the family is obvious. Freeze the scene evidence — photos, witness contacts, the medico-legal certificate — while it exists; after it is gone, no appeal recovers it.

Where the policy fits in a protection plan

Where it earns its place

  • Gig, delivery and field roles where income depends on physical presence daily
  • Breadwinners wanting a cheap top-up above the term plan's accidental-death economics
  • Homemaker cover — accidental death or permanent disability of a homemaker carries replacement-cost consequences the family feels immediately
  • Parents and in-laws above life-term entry ages (personal accident entry runs to 70)

Where it disappoints

  • Buyers treating it as a substitute for a term plan — illness kills more Indians than accidents; a ₹25 lakh PA policy pays nothing for a cancer death

  • Claimants who skip the FIR or file beyond the intimation window
  • Anyone needing cashless accident treatment — the expense cover here is reimbursement-based

Does a personal accident policy cover death by heart attack while driving?

Generally no. The accident must be the sole and direct cause — a cardiac event that causes the crash is an illness death, and pure personal accident covers exclude claims where disease is the root cause, even when the crash is violent. This boundary is the most contested in the segment; medical-event deaths belong under a term plan, which pays regardless of cause.

What does 'permanent total disability' mean in this policy?

Total and irreversible loss of use of both hands, both feet, both eyes, or sight/hearing in one ear plus a limb — the definitions sit in the policy wordings. It pays 100% of the sum insured. Partial permanent losses pay a percentage from the standard scale (one eye 50%, one hand or foot 50%, thumb 25%), which is why sum insured should be sized to partial-loss scenarios, not only to the total-loss headline.

How quickly must a personal accident claim be intimated?

Immediately in practice — within days through the insurer's call centre or portal, with the FIR obtained at once for road or public incidents. Policy intimation deadlines run to 12 months, but evidence (scene photos, witnesses, medico-legal certificate) decays in hours. Late intimation with stale evidence is the standard pattern behind rejected accident claims.

Is accidental hospitalisation cover cashless?

No — it is reimbursement against bills for treatment following an accident, subject to minimum 24-hour admission, treatment within the defined post-accident window, and plan limits. Keep the discharge summary, bills and the accident-linked diagnosis consistent; mismatched documentation between the accident narrative and the hospital record is where these claims stall.

Should I buy personal accident cover instead of a term plan?

No — they answer different questions. Accidents cause a minority of premature deaths; illness and natural causes take more lives, and only term cover pays for those. The right structure is term cover as the mortality base, health cover for treatment costs, and personal accident cover as a cheap income-protection top-up for physically dependent roles.

Can I hold more than one personal accident policy?

Yes — personal accident covers are payable alongside each other (they are not indemnity policies in the health sense), so a base employer accident cover plus an individual policy means both pay on the same admissible permanent disability or death claim. What multiple policies do NOT do is lift the product past its purpose: stacking three accident policies still pays nothing for an illness death, and each insurer will still value partial disability on the standard percentage scale against its own sum insured.
