LIC Digi Term Plan 876 Review 2026: Level vs Increasing SA, Non-Smoker Rates, Claim Math

LIC online-only term, stripped to the death claim: two benefit structures, two rate categories, and a claim formula worth reading before the first premium.

LIC sells its pure term insurance two ways now, and the split is deliberate: Yuva Term (Plan 875) runs through agents and branches, Digi Term (Plan 876, UIN 512N356V02) is online-only through licindia.in — same non-par, pure-risk architecture, priced a notch lower because no intermediary sits in the middle. Launched 5 August 2024 alongside LIC's credit-life pair, Digi Term is the state insurer's direct answer to the online term market the private players own: sums assured from ₹50 lakh to ₹5 crore, two death-benefit structures, genuine non-smoker pricing, and high-sum-assured rebates reaching into the thirties. This review works the product document: the Level-versus-Increasing choice that most buyers get wrong, the premium-mode arithmetic, the cotinine-test fine print, and what the claim actually pays.

The two death benefit options — the choice that shapes the policy

Digi Term pays no maturity benefit; it is a death-claim instrument, and the entire design question is what the death benefit looks like over time:

Option I — Level Sum Assured

  • Death benefit equals the basic sum assured for every year of the term
  • Simple, cheapest, and the right default when cover is sized to a static liability
  • Pairs well with loans that amortise down — the cover and the debt shrink together

Option II — Increasing Sum Assured

  • Basic sum assured for years 1–5; then +10% of basic sum assured each year from year 6 through year 15
  • Reaches 200% of basic sum assured in year 15, and stays at 2X for the rest of the term
  • Fights inflation on the cover itself — the only term design that does so mechanically
  • Irrevocable: the option chosen at proposal cannot be swapped later

₹ death benefit (Option II)

Growth is 10% of BASIC sum assured per year from year 6 to 15, then flat at 2X. The death claim itself is the highest of: 7× annualised premium, 105% of total premiums paid, or the absolute amount above — for single premium, higher of 125% of premium or the absolute amount.

The pricing question settles itself with one line of the brochure: Option II costs more per year, and the cover it eventually delivers is double. For a 30-year-old buying to a 55-year-old retirement, the years that matter for family protection — when the children are dependent and the loan is largest — are exactly the years the increase is compounding. For buyers whose exposure genuinely amortises (a home loan burning down, a business partner buying out), Level is the honest fit. The option is locked at inception, so model the family's dependency curve before the first premium, not after.

Premium modes, women's rates, and the smoker test

Three pricing levers sit inside Digi Term, and each is a real rupee:

  • Payment mode flexibility — single premium, limited premium, or regular premium across your chosen policy term. Single premium removes lapse risk entirely and suits volatile-income buyers; regular premium maximises the death benefit per annual rupee for salaried buyers; limited premium (pay over a shorter window, say to age 60) sits between and is usually the value sweet spot.
  • Special rates for women — LIC prices female lives lower by default on this table; the discount is built into the grid rather than being a negotiable extra.
  • Non-smoker versus smoker rates — two separate rate categories, and the discriminator is the urinary cotinine test (a nicotine metabolite). This is not a checkbox declaration: the finding of the test decides the rate category, and a failed test reclassifies the premium retroactively. Occasional social smokers frequently assume they can declare non-smoker; the chemistry does not agree, and the reclassification surfaces at claim time more often than at proposal.

High sum assured rebates cut tabular premiums substantially — up to 16% in the ₹1–2 crore band and into the mid-20s and low-30s at ₹2 crore and above for younger ages under Option II — which is why the ₹50 lakh minimum is worth stretching past for anyone who can afford the cover: the marginal lakh of cover costs less than proportionate rupees of premium. Entry ages run 18 to 45, and the sum assured ladder tightens as it climbs (₹1 lakh multiples up to ₹75 lakh, ₹25 lakh multiples to ₹1.5 crore, ₹50 lakh above that) — beyond ₹5 crore is underwriting case-by-case with reinsurer sign-off.

Instalment death benefit — the option nominees rarely hear about

At claim stage, Digi Term allows the death benefit to be paid in instalments over 5, 10 or 15 years instead of a lump sum — an option the life assured can exercise during their lifetime for full or part of the benefit. The economics are transparent: money left with LIC earns a guaranteed rate, so a family receiving ₹50 lakh over 15 years gets the headline plus interest; the counter-case is real too — a family with a business opportunity or a loan payoff needs the lump. The exercise right belongs to the life assured, which makes it a planning tool rather than a claim-time negotiation; record the preference with the nominee so the choice survives you.

Worked case: a 32-year-old buying ₹2 crore

Numbers make the design concrete. Take a 32-year-old salaried professional in Bengaluru, married, one dependent parent, a ₹45 lakh home loan, and a target cover of ₹2 crore (income multiple plus loan clearance plus education provision). Under Digi Term, the mechanics run as follows. Option II is chosen, because the dependency curve peaks years 15–30 from now — exactly when the increasing sum assured reaches and holds 2× cover, so the protection tracks the family's exposure instead of freezing at a 2026 number. Limited premium payment — paying to age 60 — keeps the annual outgo affordable while removing the risk of a renewal lapse in a lean year. The high sum assured rebate applies at the ₹2 crore band (16% for ages up to 30 under Option II, 14% for 31–50), and a cotinine-tested non-smoker declaration pulls the base rate into the lower category; together they compound into a materially cheaper policy than the tabular quote a walk-in buyer assumes. At claim time the formula self-checks: seven times the annualised premium on a ₹2 crore absolute benefit is not competitive — the absolute amount dominates, so the family receives ₹2 crore (rising under Option II to ₹4 crore by policy year 15) whenever the claim is admissible. The two failure modes to design out at proposal: a misdeclared smoker status (the cotinine test will find it) and a sum assured that "feels large" today but is frozen while the loan and school fees are not — which is precisely what the Option II increase exists to solve.

The claim: what the family actually receives

Because the product is non-par and non-linked, the death benefit is guaranteed and fixed — no bonus, no surplus participation, no market linkage. What the nominee receives is Sum Assured on Death: under regular and limited premium, the highest of seven times annualised premium, 105% of total premiums paid to date, or the absolute amount (the Option I or II figure above); under single premium, the higher of 125% of the single premium or the absolute amount. For a ₹1 crore policy at ordinary ages, the absolute amount dominates comfortably — the seven-times rule only bites on very short durations between purchase and death. Digi Term against the alternatives, honestly

Where Digi Term wins

  • The LIC brand at the claim moment — for families who need the state insurer's name on the document
  • Genuine non-smoker pricing with a test behind it — healthy lives pay what the risk tables say
  • Option II's mechanical inflation fight on the cover itself
  • Women's rate grid, high-₹ rebates, and instalment payout option

Where it concedes

  • No rider menu: no critical illness rider, no accident benefit rider on the base table — protection riders need separate policies
  • Online-only execution: buyers who want an agent in the room for claims assistance must take Yuva Term (875) at offline pricing
  • Ages 46+ cannot enter at all — the table stops at 45
  • Private online term plans still price sharper at the same cover for the healthiest lives

The correct frame for a pure term purchase is the claim, not the premium: ₹20,000 a year separates from ₹30,000 a year over a working life, but only one number matters in the worst year of your family's life. Read the death-claim clause, size the cover to today's exposure, and pick the option that fits your dependency curve — then leave it alone. What is the difference between LIC Digi Term and Yuva Term?

The product architecture is the same — both are non-par, non-linked pure risk plans from LIC launched August 2024. Digi Term (876) is sold online only through licindia.in and prices lower because no intermediary commission is embedded; Yuva Term (875) is the offline twin available through LIC's agents and branches. Choose by how you want claims assistance delivered, then compare the quote at your age.

Level or Increasing sum assured — which should I choose?

Option I (Level) fits liabilities that shrink — an amortising home loan, a static family provision. Option II (Increasing) adds 10% of basic sum assured each year from year 6 to year 15, reaching double cover, and then stays flat — it fights inflation on the protection itself, which matters most in the decades of maximum family dependency. The choice is irrevocable after inception, so model the dependency curve first.

Does Digi Term pay anything on survival?

No. There is no maturity benefit — the policy pays nothing if you survive the term. This is a pure protection instrument, which is exactly why the premium per lakh of cover is a fraction of endowment or money-back pricing. Treat the premium as an expense that buys your family's claim, never as an investment.

How does LIC verify non-smoker status in Digi Term?

Through the urinary cotinine test — a nicotine metabolite test whose finding determines the rate category. It is not a declaration-based discount: social smokers who declare non-smoker risk reclassification and retroactive premium adjustment, and the discrepancy can surface at claim stage. Declare honestly; the smoker rate is still competitive against the misdeclaration risk.

Can the death benefit be paid in instalments?

Yes — the life assured may opt, during their lifetime, for the death benefit to be paid in instalments over 5, 10 or 15 years instead of a lump sum, for the full benefit or part of it. Amounts left with LIC earn a guaranteed interest rate in the interim. It suits families needing steady income over a windfall; record the preference and inform the nominee.

What happens if I stop paying Digi Term premiums?

The policy lapses — and a pure term plan has no surrender value or paid-up value to fall back on; the cover simply ends. Revival within the grace-plus-revival window restores the policy on payment of arrears with interest, subject to underwriting. The practical defence is structural, not administrative: choose a limited-premium window you can carry through a job loss, keep the annual outgo inside the 5–10% of income band that survives lean years, and treat the premium as a fixed obligation ranked with the loan EMI rather than with discretionary spending.
