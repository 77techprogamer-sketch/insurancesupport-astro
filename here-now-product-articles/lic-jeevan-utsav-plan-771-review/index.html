LIC Jeevan Utsav Plan 771 Review 2026: The 10% Income Promise, Real IRR and Claim Behaviour

Published 15 October 2026 · Independent product analysis · Reading time ~7 minutes

LIC Jeevan Utsav (Plan 771) is the plan LIC's own sales pitch leads with in 2026: pay premiums for a limited period, then receive 10% of your sum assured as guaranteed income for life. Competitor reviews correctly note the income is 10% of sum assured — not 10% of your money — and put the IRR around 5.4–5.7%. What those reviews miss is how the plan behaves at claim time, which is where this policy's real design shows.

Two income options, one trap

Jeevan Utsav offers Regular Income and Flexi Income. The trap is arithmetic, not paperwork: a ₹10 lakh policy pays ₹1,00,000 a year — a real income stream, but the premium that generated it was far larger. Before buying, calculate the IRR from LIC's benefit illustration; anything sold to you as "10% return" is describing the payout ratio, not the investment yield.

How claims work on Plan 771

Death claim (nominee)

The nominee receives the death benefit plus all accrued guaranteed additions. Because the plan is non-participating, there is no bonus variation to dispute — the calculation is deterministic, which makes disputed death claims on this plan relatively rare. Documentation mirrors other LIC death claims: original bond (or duplicate via indemnity), death certificate, nominee KYC, and bank details. Claims inside the first three policy years are investigated as on any new LIC policy.

Claim during the income phase

This is the part competitors don't cover. If the life assured dies during the income payout phase, the nominee's death benefit is reduced by income already paid, subject to the 105%-of-premiums floor. Families who assumed the full sum assured would be payable after years of income drawdown are frequently shocked — plan for this explicitly.

Income stopped or claim delayed? LIC sometimes disputes whether an income instalment was claimed before or after a death, or misapplies the 105% floor. These are appealable. See the LIC claim rejection appeal guide for the escalation sequence.

Verdict

Buy Jeevan Utsav if you want certainty — a defined income floor for life, backed by India's largest insurer — and accept ~5.5% IRR for that certainty. Do not buy it as a 10%-return product. Existing holders: your contract is deterministic; keep the original bond safe and review the nominee entry, because the death benefit calculation depends on the sum assured on record, not on the premium you paid.

Frequently asked questions

Is LIC Jeevan Utsav's 10% income a 10% return?

No. The 10% is of your basic sum assured, not of premiums paid. A ₹10 lakh policy pays ₹1,00,000 per year. The actual IRR is typically 5.4% to 5.7% depending on how long the income is received.

What does the nominee receive if the policyholder dies during the income phase?

The death benefit is adjusted for income instalments already paid, subject to a floor of 105% of all premiums paid. Accrued guaranteed additions are added to the death benefit.

Is LIC Jeevan Utsav Plan 771 surrenderable or eligible for a loan?

Yes to both, after one full year's premium has been paid. Early surrender materially reduces the value received; the surrender value accounts for guaranteed additions accrued to date.

How is Jeevan Utsav different from Jeevan Anand?

Jeevan Utsav (771) is a whole-life guaranteed-income plan with limited premium payment and no participating bonus. Jeevan Anand (715) is an endowment plan with bonuses and a maturity payout, plus free lifetime cover on the base sum assured after maturity.
