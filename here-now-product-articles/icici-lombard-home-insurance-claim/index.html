ICICI Lombard Home Insurance Claim 2026: 48-Hour Surveyor SLA, Documents, Payouts

Surveyor within 48 hours, report in seven days, payment after the voucher — and the four document gaps where property claims actually stall.

ICICI Lombard's Complete Home Protect covers both the building and what sits inside it — owned or rented — and its claim process is built around one hard number the insurer publishes proudly: a surveyor on site within 48 hours of an authenticated intimation. Beyond that, the chain runs final survey report within seven days, claim processed within seven days of complete documents, settlement letter with a discharge voucher, and NEFT once you return the signed voucher. This review walks that chain against the second timeline printed in the policy wordings (24-hour site report, 72-hour interim report), the document set for each loss type — fire, flood, theft with FIR and non-traceable report, breakage — and how the sum insured actually converts into rupees, including the depreciation that catches content claims out.

What the policy covers — and the exclusions that decide claims

Structure and content cover under one contract: the building insured at reconstruction value (area × construction rate per square foot as on the date of taking the policy, land cost excluded) and contents — furniture, appliances, fixtures, jewellery and personal effects — at market value less depreciation for age. Three exclusions from the right-hand column deserve special attention because they are behavioural, not accidental. The 30-day unoccupied rule: leave the house for a spell past 30 consecutive days without telling the insurer and a perfectly genuine theft or burst-pipe claim becomes repudiable — intimation is a one-line email and it resets the cover. The seepage family: slow leaks, damp and seepage are maintenance issues, so the damage a plumber would have prevented sits outside cover; sudden and accidental bursts are covered, which makes the plumber's invoice part of your evidence kit. The device carve-outs: laptops and mobiles under base wordings are the most-claimed, least-covered household items — check whether your edition carries them or an add-on that does.

The claim chain: published SLAs, stage by stage

ICICI Lombard publishes the sequence on its own help pages, and the wordings print a second, tighter internal variant. Both are reproduced here because the claim conversation starts with "you said 48 hours".

Two mechanics follow from that chain. First, the 48-hour clock starts at authenticated intimation — policy details and your account of the event — so have the policy number, the loss date and a one-line narrative ready when you call. Second, the seven-day processing windows are conditional on documents: "provided we have received all documents" is the insurer's own phrasing, which makes the document set below the schedule you actually control. Where the loss is large, the wordings variant allows an on-account payment while the final assessment runs — ask for it explicitly if cash flow matters after a major event; nobody offers it automatically.

The FIR-plus-NTR pairing in the theft row is the one people miss: an FIR alone records the complaint; the investigation report (or its non-traceable closure) evidences the outcome the wordings ask for. Start both at once — police timelines, not insurer timelines, usually set the claim's total duration.

How the sum insured becomes a settlement figure

Building sum insured: area in square feet × construction rate per square foot as at policy inception — land excluded — which is why a ₹1 crore market-value flat might carry a ₹40–60 lakh building sum insured and still be correctly insured. Insure too low and a reconstruction shortfall emerges at claim time; the surveyor tests the declared basis against current rates.

Contents: market value of a comparable item today, less depreciation for the age of the item. The worked example below is the shape of most content claims — the surveyor assesses, depreciation applies per the schedule, and the gap between "what I paid" and "what I get" is the depreciation column:

Three practical consequences: keep the inventory (invoices, photographs, warranty cards) current at renewal — the surveyor pays what evidence supports; check the contents sum insured against today's values every couple of years, because gadget inflation outruns declarations; and remember that building age matters — structures beyond about 50 years are taken case-to-case, and kutcha or basement constructions fall outside scope in most editions.

Timing traps at purchase and at claim

  • The seven-day start gap. The policy start date for home insurance is seven days from the online transaction date — a fire on day four after purchase is not covered. Buy when you are between tenants or before renovation starts, not after the contractor arrives.
  • Vacancy disclosure. Unoccupied beyond 30 consecutive days without notice to the company voids the claim for that period. One email before a long trip restores the position.
  • Renewal gaps. Maximum policy term is five years, but annual renewal keeps declarations honest — a five-year policy bought once and never re-valued drifts below inflation every year it runs.
  • Selling the property. The policy is non-transferable; on sale it can be cancelled with pro-rata premium refund for the balance period — the buyer needs their own cover from day one of possession.

When the settlement is wrong or late

The chain above is a published commitment, not a contract remedy — so if the surveyor's report understates the loss, the seven-day processing window slips without explanation, or the discharge voucher arrives with an approved amount you dispute, do not sign it: a signed discharge voucher closes the claim. Instead, request the surveyor's report and the assessment breakup in writing, reply with invoice evidence item by item, and if the answer does not move, escalate: insurer grievance cell with the claim reference, then IRDAI's grievance escalation, then the Insurance Ombudsman — property claims of ordinary household size sit comfortably within ombudsman limits. What holds a claim up

  • Missing FIR / NTR in theft claims — the file cannot close without the police outcome
  • No purchase invoices for claimed contents; assessment collapses to estimated depreciated values
  • Vacancy past 30 days undisclosed, or seepage framed as flood
  • Unsigned claim form or policy copy absent — small things that stop the seven-day clock
  • Signing the discharge voucher before checking the assessment breakup

What keeps it clean

  • Photograph the damage before moving anything; keep the scene intact for the surveyor
  • Inventory with invoices and photos, refreshed at each renewal
  • Vacancy and renovation intimated in writing before they happen
  • Claim intimated within 24 hours with policy number and narrative ready
  • On-account payment requested explicitly for large losses

The surveyor visit: what to have ready in the room

The surveyor is an assessor, not an adversary — but the visit itself shapes the final figure, so treat it as a working session with a checklist. Have the policy schedule, the claim intimation reference and the police papers (for theft) on the table before they arrive, plus the purchase invoices or warranty cards for the larger claimed items, and photographs taken before anything was moved. Walk the surveyor through each damaged room in damage order rather than chronology, pointing out structural impact separately from contents: structural findings feed the building assessment, contents feed the depreciated-value schedule, and conflating the two slows the report. If an item's invoice is missing, a card statement line or an old photograph with the object visible is the fallback evidence — submit it during the visit, not after the report is drafted. Finally, ask one question before they leave: which documents does the file still show as outstanding? The seven-day processing window starts only when the set is complete, and knowing the gap on the day of the survey removes a week from the claim.

How fast does ICICI Lombard send a surveyor for a home claim?

On authenticated intimation — call 1800 2666 with policy details — the insurer's published sequence puts a surveyor on site within 48 hours, the final survey report within 7 days, claim processing within 7 days of complete documents, then a settlement letter and discharge voucher, with NEFT after you return the signed voucher. The policy wordings variant prints tighter internals (site report in 24 hours, interim report in 72 hours, on-account payment where justified).

Why is my contents settlement lower than what the items cost me?

Contents are insured and assessed at market value less depreciation for the age of the item — an eight-year-old refrigerator settles near scrap-adjusted worth, not its shelf price. The protection against a brutal gap is evidence and currency: purchase invoices, photographs and a contents sum insured re-checked against replacement values at each renewal.

Do I need an FIR for a burglary claim?

Yes — the standard document set is the claim form, policy copy, FIR, and the police Investigation Report (the non-traceable report where property is not recovered), plus supplier and repairer invoices and a list of stolen articles with purchase proof. File the FIR and press for the investigation outcome immediately: the police timeline, not the insurer's, usually sets the claim's total duration.

What does the 30-day unoccupied rule actually mean?

If the home stands unoccupied for more than 30 consecutive days and the insurer was not informed, claims arising in that window are repudiable under the standard exclusions. A short written intimation before a long trip or extended stay elsewhere keeps the cover live — it costs one email and removes the most common post-vacation claim defence.

When does home insurance cover actually start?

Seven days from the online transaction date — the policy start date is printed in the schedule. Damage in that first week is not covered, which is why the purchase should precede renovation, handover or tenant move-in rather than follow it. Also check the building's age: structures older than roughly 50 years are accepted case-to-case and basement or kutcha constructions sit outside most editions.
