LIC Jeevan Labh Review 2026: Real Returns, Bonus Rates & Claim Process

Limited premiums, 25 years of cover — but what does the plan actually pay? The illustration numbers, honestly explained.

LIC's Jeevan Labh is the plan agents quote most often when a customer wants an endowment with a shorter premium runway. Competitor reviews from Policybazaar, Ditto and Nyvo all open with the same hook: you pay for fewer years than the policy runs, and the bonus keeps accruing after your premiums stop. What those reviews often bury is the number that actually matters — on LIC's own benefit illustration, the plan's best-case scenario reaches you as 5.30% a year, and its guaranteed-only path pays 1.81%. This review walks the plan end to end — mechanics, real illustration figures, bonus history, surrender and loan rules, the Plan 936 to Plan 736 transition — and then shows you where claim support actually gets tested: the maturity and death claim paperwork.

How Jeevan Labh actually works

Jeevan Labh is a participating, non-linked, individual endowment assurance plan. You choose a policy term of 16, 21 or 25 years, but the premium-paying term is shorter than the policy term. The three standard combinations: a 16-year term with 10 years of premiums, a 21-year term with 15 years of premiums, and a 25-year term with 16 years of premiums. Entry age runs from 8 to 65 years, minimum sum assured is ₹2 lakh (accident-benefit riders change the floor), and the maximum maturity age is 75. Premiums can be paid annually, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly (monthly only under the ECS/NACH mode).

You stop paying early, but the cover and the bonus clock keep running for the full term.

Because premium-paying years are fewer than policy years, the annual premium is noticeably higher than a regular endowment of the same sum assured — that is the design, not a pricing quirk. At maturity you receive the basic sum assured plus accrued simple reversionary bonus (SRB) plus any final additional bonus (FAB), provided all due premiums are paid. Death during the term pays the higher of the sum assured, 125% of total premiums paid, or the maturity amount, plus accrued bonuses.

What LIC's own illustration shows — the 1.81% / 2.35% / 5.30% spread

IRDAI requires every traditional plan illustration to show two gross-return scenarios, 4% and 8%. The critical point competitors sometimes skip: the 8% is what LIC assumes it earns on the fund, not what it pays you. As a participating plan, LIC keeps the spread between gross returns and the bonus it declares. The Plan 936 to Plan 736 transition (why you may see two plan numbers)

Plan 936 was withdrawn for new sales on 1 October 2024 when the new IRDAI product-registration regime took effect, and LIC re-filed the plan as Plan 736, UIN 512N304V03. For anyone buying now, Plan 736 is the Jeevan Labh on offer; older reviews, illustrations and YouTube explainers still say "936". Existing 936 policies continue exactly as issued — plan renumbering does not change a single contract term, bonus rate or claim outcome. If your policy documents say 936 and a new article says 736, you are reading about the same product.

Bonus, surrender and loan mechanics

Bonus on a participating plan is declared annually out of LIC's valuation surplus. Once declared it is guaranteed — it cannot be clawed back — but the rate is set each year, so a 25-year policy will see many declarations. For maturity claims, LIC pays SRB accrued over the term plus FAB if the policy qualifies (FAB is normally declared for longer-duration policies). Two practical rules trip up policyholders mid-term:

  • Surrender: a paid-up or surrendered Jeevan Labh pays the guaranteed surrender value (a % of total premiums paid, rising with policy age, roughly 30–75% depending on duration) plus the surrender value of accrued bonus (a % of bonus, typically 15–30%). Surrendering after the minimum premium conditions are met is the only exit worth considering; before that, you get nothing.
  • Loan: policy loans become available after premiums have been paid for at least 3 consecutive years, up to 80–90% of the surrender value. The loan rate resets half-yearly and is charged on the outstanding balance whether or not you service it — an unserviced loan compounds against the maturity payout.

How Jeevan Labh compares with Jeevan Anand — the one real difference

At similar premium levels, the Jeevan Labh vs Jeevan Anand question is decided by a single structural difference. Jeevan Labh's cover ends at maturity; you collect sum assured plus bonuses and the contract closes. Jeevan Anand (the whole-life variant) continues cover to age 100 after maturity — you still get the maturity amount at the chosen term, and the death benefit then pays to the policyholder's nominees at age 100 or earlier death. Labh suits someone who wants premiums to stop at, say, 56 and a clean payout at 61; Anand suits someone who wants the maturity money plus protection that never lapses. Where Jeevan Labh is strong

  • Premiums stop 5–9 years before maturity — cash-flow friendly for salaried buyers
  • Tax-free maturity under Section 10(10D) (premium within the 20% of SA limits under old regime rules)
  • Declared SRB is guaranteed once credited; endowment-class bonus rates have been stable across cycles
  • Loan after 3 years; LIC claim settlement 98.15% (FY 2024-25, individual death claims)

Where it is weak

  • Real return: guaranteed-only path is ~1.8% p.a.; even the 8% gross scenario reaches you at 5.30%
  • Surrender in the early years is punitive — 30%+ of premiums forfeited
  • No accident cover unless the rider is bought separately; no inflation indexation
  • Bonus is declared annually — competitors' "₹54/1000 forever" framing is not a contract term

Who should buy it — and who shouldn't

Buy Jeevan Labh if you have a defined rupee goal (child's education, home construction final payment) where capital safety matters more than return, you want the discipline of a limited-premium schedule, and the tax-free maturity under Section 10(10D) is doing real work in your plan. Do not buy it as your retirement engine, your only life cover, or as a substitute for term insurance — ₹1 crore of pure term cover costs a healthy 30-year-old a fraction of what this plan's premium buys in cover. The efficient structure most fee-only advisors suggest: cheap term cover for protection, PPF or equity for growth, and an endowment like this only for the goal where you cannot afford market risk. And whichever agent you buy from, insist on the printed benefit illustration before signing — the 4% and 8% columns are the same document the agent gets.

The claim stage is where policies get tested

Buying is the easy half. The half where LIC policyholders actually lose money is the claim: maturity claims where the payout differs from the agent's verbal commitment, and death claims where nominees run into premium-discrepancy or non-disclosure objections. Two habits prevent most of it. First, at issue, verify the proposal form details — occupation, income, existing cover declarations and health history — because non-disclosure is the single largest ground for claim repudiation under Section 45 of the Insurance Act. Second, at claim time, file with the complete document set: original policy bond, maturity claim discharge vouchers, death claim needs the death certificate, nominee ID and bank proof. LIC's FY 2024-25 individual death-claim settlement stands at 98.15% with most claims settled within 30 days, so properly documented claims are rarely the problem — undocumented ones are. Is LIC Jeevan Labh Plan 936 still available in 2026?

Plan 936 was withdrawn for new sales on 1 October 2024 and re-filed under the new IRDAI product rules as Plan 736 (UIN 512N304V03). Existing 936 policies continue unchanged; new purchases happen on Plan 736 with the same structure.

What return does Jeevan Labh actually give?

On LIC's own benefit illustration (₹2 lakh SA, 25-year term, 16-year premium term): the guaranteed-only path returns about 1.81% p.a., the 4% gross scenario about 2.35% p.a., and the 8% gross scenario about 5.30% p.a. Only the sum assured is contractually guaranteed.

Can I surrender Jeevan Labh after 3 years?

Yes, once the minimum premium conditions are met (typically 3 full years' premiums, 2.5 for some terms). You receive the guaranteed surrender value (roughly 30–75% of premiums paid, rising with policy age) plus the surrender value of accrued bonus. Early surrender forfeits a large share of premiums.

Does Jeevan Labh continue after maturity?

No. Unlike Jeevan Anand, Jeevan Labh's cover ends at maturity — you receive sum assured plus accrued bonuses and the policy closes. If you want post-maturity whole-life cover, Jeevan Anand is the variant that provides it.

Premium levels and the rider question

There is no single Jeevan Labh premium — it is priced by entry age, sum assured, term and premium mode, and the same cover can differ 15–20% between a 25-year-old and a 35-year-old buyer. What does not change: the limited-premium design means each year's cheque is materially larger than a plain endowment's for identical sum assured. The accident benefit rider (covering accidental death and, in some variants, permanent total disability) adds a few percentage points to premium and is worth buying on this plan specifically, because the base cover otherwise carries no accident uplift — and accident claims, unlike disease-related disputes, are almost never contested when the FIR or medical record is in order. The critical illness rider is more expensive and more restrictive; buyers serious about CI cover usually get better terms from a standalone health-side CI plan than by bolting it onto an endowment.

On taxation: premiums qualify under Section 80C within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit, and maturity proceeds are tax-free under Section 10(10D) as long as the annual premium stays within the statutory cap (20% of sum assured for policies issued before FY 2023-24; the current regime applies its own conditions). GST at 4.5% applies to first-year premium and 2.25% thereafter — visible on the receipt but embedded in the quoted figure. One documentation habit pays off decades later: keep the premium receipts and the original bond together, because the maturity claim set is only complete with both.
