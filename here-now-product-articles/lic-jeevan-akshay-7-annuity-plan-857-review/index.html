LIC Jeevan Akshay VII Plan 857 Review 2026: Annuity Rates, 10 Options, Tax Truth

Ten options, one irreversible decision: what each annuity variant guarantees, what it costs in monthly income, and what the tax office keeps of it.

Money buys a pension in exactly one move in this product: you hand LIC a lump sum today, and the annuity starts from the very next payment cycle — no vesting age, no accumulation phase, no market dependence. LIC's Jeevan Akshay VII (Plan 857, UIN 512N337V04) is the state insurer's immediate annuity desk, and it is quietly one of the most configurable retirement-income products in India: ten annuity variants (Options A through J), a minimum purchase of ₹1 lakh, and rates that climb steeply with age — above 9% p.a. at 60 on the plain life option, past 18% at 80. This review works the option grid, the actual rates LIC's calculator throws at different ages, the spouse-continuation arithmetic, and the tax treatment that makes or breaks the net income.

The ten options, grouped by what they guarantee

Ten sounds like a lot until you sort them by the three questions every annuity buyer actually has — who gets paid, for how long, and what happens to the principal:

The structural trade is visible in the table itself: every rupee of security you buy — a guarantee period, a continuing spouse, the principal back — reduces the cheque that arrives each month. Option A pays the most and leaves nothing; Option J leaves everything and pays the least. That is not a design quirk; it is the price of insurance mechanics working honestly, and the right choice depends on whether the annuity is meant as an income floor for a single life or as a family maintenance instrument.

The rates, by age — where the real decision lives

Annuity rates rise with age because life expectancy shortens — the arithmetic is cold and the differences are large. LIC's own premium calculator, quoting Option A (plain life annuity) on a ₹10 lakh purchase, moves through these indicative rates:

The same purchase at 60 through Option F — life annuity with return of purchase price — yields roughly ₹6.73% p.a., and Option G's escalating stream starts near 7.49% and grows 3% simple every year. So at 60, plain Option A buys about ₹92,700 a year; buying the spouse a 100% continuation (Option I) costs roughly a rupee in eight; and handing the principal back to heirs (Option J) costs nearly a quarter of the cheque. Two refinements matter at purchase: the payout mode (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly) shifts the effective annual rate, and higher purchase slabs carry better rates — the rate card, not a relationship manager, decides both.

Spouse continuation, and the arithmetic families get wrong

Options H, I and J exist for one scenario: the annuitant dies first, and the spouse's income floor must survive them. The instinct of most couples is to take the bigger cheque (Option A) and "manage" — a bet on who dies first, priced at 100% of the surviving spouse's income. The option you choose is locked for life. There is no surrender-value comfort later on an immediate annuity in the way an endowment buyer expects — this is a consumed product, not a savings pot. Immediate versus deferred: why 857 is only half the retirement-income question

Jeevan Akshay VII answers "I have the money and I need income now". The other retirement-income question — "I am still earning, but I want a pension locked at, say, 60" — belongs to LIC's deferred annuity family (Jeevan Vandana, New Jeevan Nidhi and their peers), and the two are worth reading together because the rate arithmetic differs sharply. An immediate annuity at 55 locks today's rate for a 55-year-old life expectancy; a deferred purchase at 55 pays into an accumulation phase for five years and then prices the annuity at age 60 — where the rate table is materially higher (the Option A curve moves from roughly 8% at 55 to over 9% at 60). The deferral is not free: you carry the guarantee risk during the waiting years, the pension does not exist if you need income at 55, and the deferred plans' death benefit before vesting is the accumulated corpus with a floor — not the annuity itself. The clean division: retirees with a corpus and a gap between expenses and other income should price the immediate route first; working 45–55-year-olds who can wait should model the deferred route and compare the eventual rate at the vesting age they will actually reach. Where both apply — a retiree with surplus cash and years of patience — splitting across both keeps some income flowing now and some rate growth later. Either way, the annuity rate card at the age you actually start is the only number that matters, and every other conversation is decoration around it.

Tax: the part the brochure keeps short

Annuity income is taxable as income from other sources in the hands of the recipient, at their slab rate — every rupee, every year, with no 80C-style deduction on the purchase (Section 80CCC deduction is available only for annuity plans bought out of taxable income in specific circumstances, and a lump-sum-funded purchase generally does not qualify). The tax-free component: the principal returned under Options F and J is a return of your own purchase price and is not income again; it is the stream that is taxed. Retirees often net 20–30% less than the headline rate suggests once slabs and cess are applied — model the after-slab number, not the brochure number. Where the purchase amount comes from a gratuity or provident fund corpus, the interplay of Sections 10 and 80CCC deserves a chartered accountant's hour before the cheque is written, not after.

Where Jeevan Akshay VII fits — and where it doesn't

Where it earns its place

  • Retirees converting a known corpus into a guaranteed floor that cannot outlive them
  • Couples buying Option I/J for a spouse-proof income (the true insurance use)
  • Non-earners with a lump sum — inheritance, property sale — wanting administration-free income
  • Higher ages: at 70–80 the rates (12%–18%+) beat what any bond ladder offers unconditionally

Where it misleads

  • Buyers chasing "returns": inflation eats a flat annuity for decades — Option G's 3% simple rise barely tracks long-run CPI
  • Anyone needing liquidity: the principal is gone at purchase; emergencies have no withdrawal route
  • Young earners: at 30–40, locking money at ~7% for 40+ years is usually the wrong trade versus term cover plus invested corpus
  • Tax-bracket denial: post-slab income can fall below a simple debt fund with better liquidity

The honest frame for Plan 857 is insurance, not investment: you are buying an income that survives you, priced fairly, with no upside. What is the minimum purchase amount for Jeevan Akshay VII?

₹1 lakh as a single premium. Higher purchase slabs attract better annuity rates, and the payout mode you choose (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly) also shifts the effective annual rate — both are worth checking on LIC's calculator before committing, since the option and mode are locked for life.

Which annuity option should a 60-year-old choose?

It depends on who depends on the income. Option A maximises the cheque for a single life with nothing after death; Option I continues 100% to a spouse at a bounded cost; Options F and J return the principal to heirs at further cost. Couples with a non-earning spouse should price Option I first; single retirees with no legacy motive often land on A or G.

Is the annuity income from Plan 857 taxable?

Yes — the annuity stream is taxable each year as income in the recipient's hands at their slab rate, with cess. The purchase price returned under Options F and J is your own capital coming back and is not taxed again. Model the after-slab figure: retirees commonly net 20–30% below the headline rate.

Does Jeevan Akshay VII beat a bank fixed deposit for retirement income?

On raw yield at older ages, usually yes — 9%+ at 60 and into double digits at 70+ versus prevailing deposit rates, with a guarantee the rate will never change. On flexibility, deposits win: the annuity's principal is consumed at purchase with no surrender, no withdrawal, and no inflation indexation (Option G's 3% simple increase only partly offsets). Many retirees split: part annuity for the floor, part liquid for the unknown.

What happens to the purchase price after the annuitant dies?

It depends on the option chosen at purchase. Under Options A, G, H and I, the purchase price is not returned — it was the price of the income. Under Options F and J, the nominee receives the purchase price on death (J only on the death of the last survivor, after spouse continuation). Nominees cannot claim it under an option that never included it.

Can I buy Jeevan Akshay VII on my spouse’s life?

Yes, subject to proposal and insurable-interest rules — the annuity may run on your life or your spouse’s, and couples routinely structure Option I or J so the longer-lived spouse carries the income. Decide which life the annuity should follow before purchase: the option and the life assured are fixed for the policy’s life, and the rate differs by the annuitant’s age and gender in the rate card.
