LIC Jeevan Utsav Plan 871 Review 2026: Guaranteed Additions, Income Gap, Live Policy Choices

A whole-life plan whose guarantee is real and whose income starts later than buyers expect — the gap between those two facts is where every 871 decision lives.

LIC's Jeevan Utsav (Plan 871, UIN 512N363V01) was a whole-life savings plan with a genuine guarantee inside it: ₹40 per ₹1,000 sum assured added every year during the premium-paying term, then 10% of sum assured paid annually for life. Non-par means every figure is contractual — no bonus declared, no bonus reduced. Launched in the early-2020s product wave and withdrawn in October 2024 when the successor Plan 771 took over the slot, Plan 871 still has a large in-force book whose holders face real decisions: keep, surrender, or convert to paid-up. This review works the mechanics — the guaranteed addition, the two income options, the income-start gap that catches buyers at purchase, and what a live policy is actually worth at different ages.

The architecture: guaranteed addition, then perpetual income

Jeevan Utsav runs in two phases. During the premium-paying term (PPT), a Guaranteed Addition of ₹40 per ₹1,000 basic sum assured accrues every policy year — fixed in the bond, immune to interest rates and LIC's investment performance. After the PPT ends, the policy converts into an income engine: 10% of basic sum assured payable every year for life.

The Guaranteed Addition, worked

₹40 per ₹1,000 per year sounds modest until it compounds across a PPT. On a ₹10 lakh sum assured, each policy year during the PPT adds ₹40,000 to the pot — and it stacks with the PPT length:

₹ accrued guaranteed addition

₹40 per ₹1,000 SA per PPT year on a ₹10L sum assured. GA accrues only during the PPT; the death benefit keeps it (plus SA) for life afterwards.

Two reads of that chart are both correct. A longer PPT multiplies the guaranteed pot — ₹6.4 lakh of pure guarantee at PPT 16 — but it also means premiums for sixteen years and, as the next section shows, income that starts later than buyers expect. The plan's whole character comes from that trade.

Option I versus Option II: the Flexi accumulation gap

Option I (Regular Income) pays the 10% annual income unconditionally from the first income year. Option II (Flexi) lets the income sit with LIC accumulating at 5.5% p.a., with up to 75% withdrawable once per year — better when you genuinely do not need the cash, materially worse when you do.

The income-start gap is the plan's most under-discussed feature. Because LIC must build reserve for a perpetual 10% income from a limited premium base, income begins several years after the last premium: roughly a five-year gap after a 5-year PPT, about three years after PPT 10 and PPT 16. Buyers who chose a short PPT expecting income the year premiums stopped were disappointed — the PPT choice should be made by when you want income, not by when you want to stop paying.

On the numbers, Option I typically delivers an XIRR around 5.5–6.5% by age 80 under conservative bonus-free assumptions; Option II can beat that when the accumulation window is long — as a working rule, deferring ten-plus years after income starts can grow the pool 40–70% larger than the Option I cash foregone. Neither figure is a promise of market returns; both are arithmetic on guarantees, which is precisely the product's value proposition and its ceiling.

Death benefit: the two-lane floor

The death benefit is the higher of Basic SA or 7× annualised premium, plus accrued Guaranteed Additions, with an absolute floor of 105% of total premiums paid. In early PPT years the 7× multiple can exceed the basic SA materially on high-premium, short-entry-age cases; after the PPT, the sum assured plus GA (which stops accruing at PPT end but stays in the pot) carries the benefit for life. If Option II is chosen, the accumulated Flexi pool is paid out additionally on death. The 105% floor means even a year-one death never returns less than premiums paid plus five percent — a statutory protection that matters in the first-policy-years argument families sometimes face.

Live-policy decisions: keep, surrender, paid-up

Plan 871 holders cannot be "migrated" to Plan 771 — the contract stands on its own wordings. The three options after the two-year mark:

  • Keep. The GA is guaranteed and the income is guaranteed; if the policy is past the PPT or close to it, walking away discards exactly the guarantees new policies cannot replicate at your entry age.
  • Surrender. Available after 2 years with the improved surrender-value norms of the 2020–2024 era. You trade a perpetual 10% income for a one-time figure — usually poor arithmetic unless the cash has a higher-certainty use.
  • Paid-up. The middle path: the policy continues with a reduced sum assured (SA × years paid / PPT) and proportionate benefits, premiums stop. Note the denominator — the PPT, not the total term — which is why limited-pay structures like 871 reach higher paid-up values faster than regular-pay plans.

Any of the three deserves a policy-level calculation against the bond's guaranteed figures, not a rule of thumb. The Flexi pool, worked through a decade

Option II's arithmetic is simple enough to do by hand, which is how you should test any 871 illustration before committing. Take a ₹10 lakh sum assured policy whose income phase starts at ₹1,00,000 per year. Under Option I, each year simply pays the lakh out. Under Option II, each ₹1,00,000 credited sits with LIC accumulating at 5.5% p.a. Leave the pool untouched for ten income years and the closing balance is roughly ₹12.7 lakh against ₹10 lakh of credits — an uplift of about a quarter, purely from the guarantee. Every year of continued deferral adds another ₹5.5% on a larger base, which is why the gap between the two options compounds dramatically over a retirement horizon: thirty deferral years can double the pool relative to the cash taken. The counterweight is equally simple — the pool is only worth more if you do not need the money. A retiree drawing the income for living expenses should take Option I without apology; a working professional whose salary covers expenses and who is buying for the grandchildren's decade is the genuine Flexi candidate. Illustrations quoted at sale should reproduce this arithmetic exactly; if they cannot, treat the illustration — not the product — as suspect.

What the plan teaches buyers of the successor

Plan 871's design — guaranteed addition during PPT, deferred perpetual income, whole-life cover — carried directly into Plan 771, which now serves the slot. Three lessons transfer: match PPT to when you want income (the gap is structural, not a sales oversight); treat Flexi as an accumulation decision you will actually leave alone for a decade; and remember that a non-par guarantee cuts both ways — certainty above, upside nowhere. What is LIC Jeevan Utsav Plan 871?

A non-linked, non-participating whole-life savings plan with limited premium payment (PPT 5–16 years, entry age 0–65, minimum sum assured ₹5 lakh). During the PPT it adds a Guaranteed Addition of ₹40 per ₹1,000 sum assured each year; after the PPT it pays 10% of basic sum assured every year for life. Withdrawn in October 2024 and replaced by Plan 771; in-force 871 contracts continue on their original terms.

When does the income actually start after the premium-paying term?

Not immediately — a structural gap applies while LIC builds reserve for the perpetual 10% income. Roughly a five-year gap after a 5-year PPT, and about three years after PPT 10 or PPT 16. Choose the PPT by when you want income to begin, not by when you want premiums to stop; this is the single most misunderstood feature at purchase.

Option I or Option II — which income option is better?

Option I pays 10% of sum assured in cash each year, unconditionally. Option II (Flexi) defers the income with LIC accumulating it at 5.5% p.a., withdrawable up to 75% once a year. If you can genuinely leave the money for a decade or more, Flexi often ends 40–70% larger than the Option I cash foregone; if the income is for living expenses, take Option I.

Can I surrender or convert my Plan 871 policy?

Surrender and paid-up are both available after 2 years. Surrender pays the one-time surrender value under the 2020–2024 era norms; paid-up keeps the policy running with reduced sum assured (SA × years paid / PPT — the PPT denominator is why limited-pay policies reach higher paid-up values faster). There is no migration to the successor Plan 771 — each contract stands on its own bond.

What is the death benefit under Jeevan Utsav?

The higher of Basic Sum Assured or 7× annualised premium, plus accrued Guaranteed Additions, with an absolute floor of 105% of total premiums paid. Guaranteed Additions stop accruing after the PPT but stay in the benefit; under Option II the accumulated Flexi pool is additionally payable on death. At age 100 the whole-life maturity pays Basic SA plus accrued GA.

How much does the Flexi accumulation actually add?

On a ₹10 lakh sum assured policy whose income phase is ₹1,00,000 a year, leaving the Option II pool untouched for ten income years builds roughly ₹12.7 lakh against ₹10 lakh of credits — about a 25% uplift from the 5.5% p.a. guarantee alone, with every further deferral year compounding on a larger base. The uplift is only real if the cash is genuinely not needed; income meant for living expenses belongs in Option I.
