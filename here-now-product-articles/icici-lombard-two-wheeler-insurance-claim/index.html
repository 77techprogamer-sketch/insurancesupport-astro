ICICI Lombard Two-Wheeler Insurance Claim Guide 2026: IDV, Depreciation & Cashless Process

Your premium is the small number. IDV, depreciation and the 75% total-loss rule decide the size of the cheque.

Two-wheeler insurance is India's highest-volume motor product, and the claim stage is where its design flaws show. ICICI Lombard's package policy follows the standard IRDAI framework — own damage plus statutory third-party liability in one contract — but the numbers that decide your payout are not the premium; they are the Insured's Declared Value (IDV), the depreciation schedule welded to it, and the 75% total-loss threshold. This review explains how the policy wordings set your sum insured, what the claim process actually looks like at a network garage versus a reimbursement claim, and the documentation and timing rules that move claims from "approved" to "disputed".

What the package policy covers

The own-damage section indemnifies loss or damage to the insured vehicle by: explosion, self-ignition or lightning; burglary, housebreaking or theft; riot and strike; earthquake (fire and shock); flood, typhoon, hurricane, storm, tempest, inundation, cyclone, hailstorm and frost; accidental external means; malicious act; terrorist activity; transit by road, rail, inland waterway, lift, elevator or air; and landslide or rockslide. The third-party section covers legal liability for death or bodily injury to third parties (including occupants not carried for hire) and damage to third-party property, within Motor Vehicles Act limits — for two-wheelers, the unlimited personal-accident cover for the owner-driver (₹15 lakh under current IRDAI norms) and property damage liability up to ₹1 lakh. Separate personal accident cover for the owner-driver is mandatory; riding without it is a compliance gap that surfaces at exactly the wrong moment.

IDV: the sum insured that decides everything

For total-loss claims, the sum insured is not what you paid — it is the IDV: the manufacturer's listed price adjusted for age-wise depreciation. The IRDAI-schedule depreciation used to fix IDV climbs steadily with vehicle age:

How much of showroom value survives into your IDV

Beyond 5 years, IDV is set by mutual agreement between insurer and insured — negotiate this at renewal, do not accept a token figure silently.

Two consequences matter at claim time. First, a 3-year-old scooter's IDV is around 60–70% of its original price — if the accident totals it, the payout reflects that discounted value minus wreck salvage, not the replacement cost of a new vehicle. Second, the CTL trigger: if aggregate retrieval-and-repair cost exceeds 75% of IDV, the insurer treats the vehicle as constructively totally lost and pays IDV less salvage instead of repairing. Repair estimates just below 75% get repaired; just above, they get written off — which is why an honest surveyor's estimate matters more than any clause in your hands. For parts replaced in partial-loss repairs, a separate depreciation schedule applies: 50% on rubber, nylon and plastic parts, tyres, tubes and batteries; 30% on fibre-glass components; nil on glass parts; and the age-schedule rates for other parts.

Timing rules that bite: intimate the claim without avoidable delay (most wordings expect intimation immediately or within 48 hours for theft-related losses), never move the vehicle before survey unless it blocks traffic (movement needs insurer authorisation), and for theft, file the police FIR immediately and obtain the non-traceable certificate if the vehicle is not found — the theft claim cannot settle without it. Documents for reimbursement claims: claim form, FIR copy (accident/theft), repair invoice and payment proof, driving licence and RC copy, policy copy, and the surveyor's estimate — the set that most often stalls is the licence-RC pair, particularly where the rider at the time of accident was not the insured or lacked a valid licence.

No-claim bonus, and the NCB two-wheeler trap

No-claim bonus discounts the own-damage premium for claim-free years — up to 50% on two-wheelers after consecutive claim-free renewals. The trap: NCB belongs to the owner, not the vehicle, but two-wheeler owners routinely let it reset. Selling a scooter without endorsing the NCB to yourself (or to a new policy) loses years of accrued discount in one signature gap; conversely, claiming every small dent destroys an NCB worth more than the small claim. The decision rule most riders never run: if repair cost is below roughly 1.5× the annual NCB saving, paying out of pocket preserves the bonus. Also note: NCB applies only to own-damage premium — third-party liability premium is statutory and unaffected either way.

Where the policy works

  • Statutory third-party liability plus owner-driver PA cover in one annual contract
  • Network garage cashless repairs remove fronting cash for most urban riders
  • NCB up to 50% rewards claim-free years meaningfully on the OD component
  • Claim intimation through app/WhatsApp — no branch visit required to start

Where riders lose money

  • IDV-based total-loss payouts on older vehicles fall far below replacement cost
  • 75% CTL threshold decides repair-vs-write-off, not rider preference
  • Part depreciation (50% rubber/plastic, 30% fibre) reduces partial-loss settlements
  • Post-warranty non-network "familiar" garages void cashless and delay reimbursement

Renewal decisions that affect next year's claim

Three renewal choices carry into claim outcomes. IDV: accepting the lowest IDV saves a few hundred rupees and directly cuts the total-loss payout — set IDV near market value, especially in years 2–4 when depreciation bites hardest. Deductible: a voluntary per-claim deductible trims OD premium but is charged on every own-damage claim; riders who choose it should keep the deductible amount liquid. Depreciation waiver / zero-depreciation add-on: worth its price for vehicles under 5 years in cities where fibre parts and electronics dominate repair bills — it moves part-depreciation from your side to the insurer's, and on a ₹15,000–₹25,000 bumper-and-panel repair the maths closes fast. Keep the policy continuous: a lapsed own-damage cover means a break in NCB continuity and a fresh 30-day (or inspection-gated) restart at renewal.

When a motor claim goes wrong

Motor claim disputes cluster around four situations: total-loss valuations the owner considers low (challenge with current market quotations and the IDV agreed at policy inception — the schedule is contractual), partial-loss part-depreciation surprises (read the depreciation schedule before authorising repairs), theft claims stalled on non-traceable certificates (police follow-up is the only path), and cashless refusals at garages presenting themselves as network but not listed for your policy variant (verify on the insurer's current network list, not the garage's wall poster). The escalation ladder for a stuck motor claim is the same as any general-insurance dispute: insurer grievance cell first with the claim number and surveyor report, then the IRDAI Bima Bharosa portal, then the Insurance Ombudsman — our claim rejection and appeal guide lays out that sequence with the timelines that apply to each stage, and it works for motor claims exactly as it does for health.

How is the claim amount calculated on a total-loss two-wheeler claim?

On total loss or constructive total loss (repair cost exceeding 75% of IDV), the insurer pays the Insured's Declared Value fixed at policy inception (manufacturer price less age-wise depreciation) minus salvage value. Vehicle age drives the depreciation: 5% in the first 6 months rising to 50% beyond 4 years.

What depreciation applies to parts in partial-loss repairs?

50% on rubber, nylon and plastic parts, tyres, tubes and batteries; 30% on fibre-glass components; nil on glass; and the age-schedule rates on other parts. A zero-depreciation add-on shifts much of this liability to the insurer.

Can I get cashless repair at any garage?

No — cashless repairs are only at garages in the insurer's current network list for your policy variant. Verify on ICICI Lombard's official network list rather than relying on the garage's own claim; non-network repairs go on reimbursement after survey.

Does filing a small claim affect my renewal premium?

Yes — any own-damage claim resets the no-claim bonus, which can be worth up to 50% of the OD premium. For small repairs, compare repair cost against roughly 1.5× the annual NCB saving before claiming; third-party liability premium is statutory and unaffected by NCB.

Electrical and non-electrical accessories: the sum-insured gap

Accessories fitted after purchase — GPS units, alarms, custom seats, non-standard batteries — are covered only if declared and added to the policy with additional premium. At claim time, undocumented accessories are simply excluded from both IDV and settlement, and the surveyor's job is to verify what the schedule says, not what the vehicle carries. The renewal checklist that prevents this is short: list every accessory above ₹5,000 at purchase, carry the invoices, and re-verify the list at each renewal. The same principle governs the vehicle's own specifications — RC details (engine/chassis number, variant) must match the policy schedule exactly; mismatches discovered at survey stage are the second-most-common reason two-wheeler claims get delayed after licence issues.

Insurance in the first year, and what changes at renewal

New two-wheelers ship with first-year comprehensive cover built into the on-road price — the dealer settles it. The renewal decision twelve months later is where riders make their first avoidable error: defaulting to whatever the dealer or aggregator quotes without checking IDV or NCB transfer. At renewal, verify three numbers on the quote before paying: the IDV (should reflect current market value, not a token figure), the NCB percentage (should carry over from the outgoing policy — escalate at once if it reads zero without a claim), and the voluntary deductible (zero unless you have consciously chosen otherwise). Own-damage cover is optional after year one — third-party liability remains mandatory — but a vehicle financed on loan almost always carries a bank-mandated comprehensive requirement until the loan closes. Dropping OD cover to save ₹1,500–₹2,500 on a financed vehicle risks both the bank condition and, in theft or total-loss scenarios, your entire repair-or-replace position.

What "claim-free year" discipline is worth in rupees

On a ₹45,000 annual premium split roughly ₹25,000 own-damage / ₹20,000 third-party (illustrative urban scooter figures), the NCB scale from 0% to 50% moves the OD component by around ₹12,500 a year at full bonus. Three claim-free years at that level recovers more than most riders' entire annual repair budget — which is the whole argument for the "pay small dents yourself" rule. The mirror-image mistake: letting a garage push an insurance claim for a ₹3,000 scratch because "it is covered", resetting NCB worth ten times the claim. NCB loss compounds — it resets to zero, and rebuilding takes years of clean renewals. The rider's decision sheet before any claim: repair estimate × 1.5 vs annual NCB saving; claim only if the first number is bigger.

Finally, keep the policy document accessible digitally: the claim intimation app-flow asks for the policy number, and riders quoting from memory during an accident-scene call routinely give the wrong one. A screenshot of the policy schedule in the phone's offline folder costs nothing and removes the single most common first-hour delay in accident claims.
