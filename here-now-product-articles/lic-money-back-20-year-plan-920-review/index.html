LIC Money Back Plan 20 Years Review 2026: Survival Payouts, Bonus & Claim Rules

Four payouts across 20 years instead of one lump sum — what the schedule pays, what bonus actually accrues, and where claims slip.

LIC's New Money Back Plan – 20 Years is one of the few savings products where the insurer hands you cash four times instead of once. Competitor pages from Policybazaar and PolicyX all lead with that feature; almost none lead with the arithmetic consequence — because 60% of the sum assured leaves your policy in the first 15 years, the bonus clock runs on a shrinking balance, and the internal rate of return lands below LIC's plain endowment plans. This review covers the full structure (Plan 920, UIN 512N280V03), the survival-benefit schedule, what happens at maturity and on death, how bonus accrues, and the claim paperwork that decides whether the four payouts arrive on time.

Plan structure at a glance

The New Money Back Plan – 20 Years is a participating, non-linked, individual money-back plan. Entry age is 13 to 50 years, the policy term is fixed at 20 years, and the maximum maturity age is 70. Minimum sum assured is ₹1 lakh. Premiums are payable annually, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly (ECS/NACH) for the full 20-year term — unlike Jeevan Labh, there is no limited-premium shortcut here. The plan carries an optional accidental death and disability benefit rider and a terminal illness benefit under certain conditions.

The money-back schedule: 20-20-20-40

Four survival payouts are the plan's identity. At the end of the 5th, 10th and 15th policy years you receive 20% of the basic sum assured each time — these are survival benefits paid regardless of claims experience, and the policy continues with the same bonus accrual until maturity. At the end of the 20th year you receive the balance 40% of sum assured plus all accrued simple reversionary bonus and any final additional bonus.

Survival benefits are unconditional — they are paid even if claims were made in the meantime.

On a ₹5 lakh sum assured, the schedule is ₹1 lakh each at years 5, 10 and 15 (₹3 lakh out), then ₹2 lakh plus bonus at year 20. The survival instalments are paid by cheque or NEFT to the policyholder's registered bank account; if an instalment is not claimed, it accumulates with interest until the claim is made — it never expires.

Bonus mechanics on a shrinking sum assured

This is the part money-back reviews tend to skip. Simple reversionary bonus on a money-back policy is declared per ₹1,000 of basic sum assured each year, but the death/maturity benefit calculation applies the full rate to the sum assured less the survival amounts already paid out. After the first instalment leaves at year 5, bonus accrues on 80% of the original sum; after year 10, on 60%; after year 15, on 40%. LIC's money-back class bonuses have historically sat in the ₹40–₹55 per ₹1,000 range alongside the endowment class, and LIC allocated a record ₹59,726 crore to policyholders across all plans in FY 2026 — but the shrinking-base effect means the rupee of bonus your policy earns falls every time an instalment is drawn.

Bonus accrues on the balance, not the original sum assured

Illustrative — actual declared rates vary yearly. The declining base is structural to all money-back plans, from every insurer.

The practical consequence: money-back's IRR runs below a comparable plain endowment (like New Endowment Plan 714 or Jeevan Labh) for the same sum assured and premium, because bonus compounds on a decreasing base while an endowment accrues on the full base for the entire term. What you buy instead is liquidity — periodic cash that a pure endowment never gives you.

Death benefit and rider arithmetic

On death during the term, the nominee receives the full death benefit — which is the sum assured on death (not reduced by instalments already paid), plus accrued bonus, plus FAB if applicable, subject to a floor of 105% of total premiums paid. In plain terms: dying at year 12 means the family gets the complete ₹5 lakh (or chosen SA) plus bonus, even though the policyholder had already drawn ₹2 lakh in survival benefits. The optional accidental death and permanent disability benefit rider adds cover (typically equal to the base SA) in accident cases; the disability benefit is paid in instalments over a defined period rather than as one cheque.

Surrender, loan and paid-up rules

After at least 3 full years of premiums (or 2.5 years for some premium modes), the policy acquires surrender value: guaranteed surrender value (a rising percentage of premiums paid, roughly 30–50% for money-back policies depending on duration, lower than endowments because instalments have already flowed out) plus the surrender value of accrued bonus (typically 15–30%). A policy that becomes paid-up — you stop paying after the minimum period — continues at reduced benefits: all future survival instalments and the maturity amount scale down proportionally. Policy loans are available after the same 3-year threshold, up to 80–90% of surrender value, useful precisely because money-back buyers tend to be liquidity-constrained; the loan interest (reset half-yearly) compounds against you if unserviced.

Who the plan suits — and the honest alternatives

Money-back 20 suits a conservative saver who wants forced periodic liquidity with government-backed safety: parents funding a child's staged education costs, or someone replacing a maturing small savings instrument who cannot afford a 20-year lock. It suits them specifically because they would otherwise spend the lump sum. It suits them poorly if the goal is maximum corpus — the same premium directed into New Endowment (714) or Jeevan Labh delivers more at maturity because bonus accrues on the full sum assured throughout, and Sukanya Samriddhi or PPF out-return both on a tax-free basis. One further caution: the plan's maturity age cap of 70 and entry cap of 50 mean late starters have narrow windows, and agents sometimes sell it as an "investment with withdrawals" without disclosing the shrinking-base arithmetic above. Ask for the benefit illustration and compare its 4%/8% columns with a plain endowment at the same premium.

Claim paperwork: keeping the four payouts on schedule

Each survival instalment needs the original policy bond and a discharge voucher signed at the servicing branch; nominees claiming the death benefit need the death certificate, original bond, ID and bank-proof set. Most money-back claim friction is documentary rather than disputed: lost bonds (resolved via bond-trace and indemnity procedures), NEFT mandates with mismatched account details, or instalments left unclaimed for years and then disputed. LIC settled 98.15% of individual death claims in FY 2024-25, so the documented path works — the risk is arriving at the branch without the right paper. Death claims where nominee details were never updated are the most common failure point — updating the nominee at the branch (or via the LIC portal) while the policyholder is alive takes one form and prevents the most painful category of dispute, covered in our death-claim rejection recovery guide.

How much money-back do I get from LIC Money Back 20 Years?

20% of the basic sum assured at the end of each of years 5, 10 and 15 (survival benefits), then the balance 40% plus all accrued bonus and any final additional bonus at the end of year 20.

Does bonus reduce after each survival payout?

Yes. Simple reversionary bonus accrues on the sum assured less the survival amounts already paid. After the year-5 payout, bonus accrues on 80% of the original SA; after year 10, on 60%; after year 15, on 40%. This is why money-back IRRs run below plain endowment plans.

What happens if I die in the middle of the policy?

The nominee receives the full sum assured on death (not reduced by survival instalments already paid), plus accrued bonus and FAB if any, subject to a minimum of 105% of total premiums paid. Instalments already received by the policyholder do not reduce the family's payout.

Can I take a loan against a LIC money-back policy?

Yes — policy loans are available after 3 full years of premiums, up to 80–90% of the surrender value. The interest rate resets half-yearly and accrues on the outstanding balance if not serviced.

Worked example: ₹5 lakh over 20 years, cash flow by year

Numbers make the structure concrete. A 30-year-old buying ₹5 lakh sum assured pays roughly ₹30,000–₹36,000 a year depending on mode (annual mode costs least; GST of 4.5%/2.25% applies on first/subsequent years). Total outflow across 20 years is around ₹6.5–₹7.2 lakh. What comes back, in order: ₹1 lakh at year 5, ₹1 lakh at year 10, ₹1 lakh at year 15, then ₹2 lakh plus accrued bonus at year 20. With SRB declared in the ₹40–₹50 per ₹1,000 band on the declining base, total bonus on this example lands in the ₹1.5–₹2 lakh zone by maturity. The lump-sum-return buyer comparing this with New Endowment at the same premium will find the endowment's maturity figure higher by a meaningful margin — that gap is the price of the three early instalments.

Complaint patterns worth knowing before you buy

Three grievance patterns recur on money-back policies across all insurers. First, the "bonus surprise": policyholders discover at maturity that bonus accrued on the reduced base, because the agent illustrated ₹50/1,000 across all 20 years on the full sum. The illustration never lies — it simply shows a shrinking base in fine print. Second, unclaimed survival instalments: families relocate, bank mandates go stale, and instalments sit unclaimed for years; they do carry interest, but the branch paper trail to recover them is exactly the paper trail a maturity or death claim needs. Third, premature surrender after a liquidity shock, forfeiting 50%+ of premiums — the loan facility exists precisely to avoid this, and taking an 80%-of-surrender-value loan costs far less than crystallising the surrender loss. A final eligibility note: the 13-to-50 entry window and 70-year maturity cap mean the plan quietly excludes late starters, and parents buying it for minor children should note the survival instalments are paid to the policyholder, not the child — the child's money-back structure is a separate product (New Children's Money Back). Verify the nominee at issue and again at every branch touchpoint; nominee-update forms are free and take minutes, while a stale nominee record is the most common avoidable cause of death-claim delay.
