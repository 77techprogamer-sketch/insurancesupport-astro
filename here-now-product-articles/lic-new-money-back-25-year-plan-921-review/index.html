LIC New Money Back Plan 25 Years (Plan 921) Review 2026: Payout Arithmetic & Claims

Fifteen per cent every five years, the remaining 40% with bonuses at year 25 — and why the January 2025 withdrawal changes nothing for claims on existing policies.

The New Money Back Plan – 25 Years (Plan 921, UIN 512N278V02) is LIC's long-horizon money-back contract: premiums run for 20 years, the policy runs for 25, and LIC hands back 15% of the sum assured every five years along the way. It launched on 1 February 2020 — and was withdrawn from new business on 1 January 2025. That second date is the part that surprises people. Existing policies continue exactly as issued: the survival payouts, the maturity amount, the death cover and the bonus participation all run their contractual course to the last rupee. This review unpacks the payout arithmetic with worked numbers on a ₹4 lakh sum assured, the claim mechanics for each of the three benefit types, and what the withdrawal does and does not change.

One structural detail worth noticing before anything else: the premium-paying term and the policy term are different numbers. You pay for 20 years and are covered for 25. The last five years of cover come free of premium cost, which quietly improves the cash-flow profile in the back half — and it means the survival payout at year 20 arrives in the same year your premium obligation ends.

How the money-back clock actually runs

Money-back plans are often described as "part lump sum, part insurance". Plan 921 is better understood as a single sum assured, delivered in five instalments across time. The schedule below is contractual — it does not depend on market performance, bonus declarations or LIC's investment results.

Because the four survival instalments total 60% of the sum assured and maturity pays the remaining 40%, the basic sum assured is returned in full across the term — split into five pieces. The death cover, meanwhile, is computed on the basic sum assured and does not step down after a survival payout. A policyholder who has collected all four money-back instalments still leaves the nominee entitled to 125% of the original sum assured plus bonuses. The payouts are an advance distribution of the sum assured, not an erosion of it.

Worked example: ₹4,00,000 sum assured

Take a 35-year-old non-smoker buying ₹4 lakh basic sum assured for the full 25-year term. Premiums are age- and profile-dependent, so treat the benefit side as the fixed part of the contract and obtain an official quotation for the cost side. What the contract delivers:

  • Survival payouts: 15% of ₹4,00,000 = ₹60,000 at the end of years 5, 10, 15 and 20 — ₹2,40,000 returned during the policy, all of it tax-free in the hands of the recipient.
  • Maturity base: the remaining 40% = ₹1,60,000 at the end of year 25.
  • Accrued bonus: Plan 921 is a participating policy. Recent LIC declarations for participating money-back and endowment groups have run in the neighbourhood of ₹46–48 per ₹1,000 of sum assured per year. On ₹4 lakh that is roughly ₹18,400–19,200 accruing per year; averaged over the years the money is actually outstanding, an indicative accrual band of about ₹3.5–4.2 lakh over the full 25 years is a reasonable planning figure — but only the sum assured is guaranteed, bonus rates are declared annually out of valuation surplus.
  • Final additional bonus: a one-time addition at maturity if declared in that year's valuation. LIC's 2025 valuation declared FABs across several long-duration participating plans, so a 25-year policy is squarely in the zone where FABs have historically been paid — check the current declaration before projecting a number.

Indicative total inflows to the life assured across 25 years: roughly ₹7.85 lakh plus any FAB, against total premiums (age-dependent) that a 35-year-old would pay over 20 years. That places the internal rate of return in the mid-5% band — tax-free, which puts it on par with or just above post-tax fixed-deposit yields for someone in the 20–30% slab, with the insurance cover thrown in for two decades. The honest caveat: money-back structures pay out early and often, so compounding gets interrupted. For pure maturity maximisation an endowment with no interim payouts will edge ahead; for disciplined cash-flow goals — school fees at year 5, a wedding at year 15 — the schedule is the point.

Death benefit: 125% versus seven times premium

The death clause has two prongs and the nominee gets whichever is higher: 125% of basic sum assured, or 7 times the annualised premium, plus all vested simple reversionary bonuses and the FAB, if any. On the ₹4 lakh example, 125% = ₹5,00,000. If the annualised premium is around ₹28,000, seven times that is ₹1,96,000 — so the 125% prong wins comfortably and the prong is effectively a floor that protects young entrants whose premiums are small relative to cover. The seven-times prong only overtakes when premiums are unusually high for the sum assured — late entry ages with large GST-loaded premiums, for instance. Neither prong reduces because survival benefits were already paid.

For the nominee, the claim file is the standard life-insurance set: the death certificate, the policy bond (or an affidavit of non-issue if the document is lost), the prescribed claim form, nominee KYC and bank details for NEFT, and medical or police records if the circumstances invite them. Sudden or unnatural deaths routinely pull in hospital records or an FIR — that is process, not suspicion. What the 1 January 2025 withdrawal changes — and what it does not

Plan 921 was closed to new business from 1 January 2025. For a policyholder holding one, nothing in the contract moved: premium due dates, survival payout dates, maturity date, bonus eligibility and claim entitlements all continue as issued. LIC continues to service in-force policies on original terms — withdrawal stops sales, it does not amend contracts. Three practical consequences remain worth stating plainly:

  • Keep paying. The 20-year premium commitment only protects you if the policy stays in force. A lapse stops bonuses and suspends the death cover; standard LIC revival rules give a two-year window from the first unpaid premium to revive with arrears plus interest, subject to evidence of continued insurability. After the revival window the contract is gone for good.
  • Your payouts are still automatic. Survival benefits are paid on the due dates without any claim filing — but only if your bank mandate and KYC with LIC are current. A stale account number is the most common reason a year-15 payout goes astray; the fix is a servicing request, not a claim.
  • You cannot buy a fresh one. New buyers choosing between LIC's money-back options now land on the 20-year Plan 920 — which pays 20% at years 5/10/15 and 40% plus bonuses at year 20 — reviewed in detail in our LIC New Money Back 20 Years (Plan 920) review — or the limited-premium endowment Jeevan Labh Plan 736, or LIC's current-year menu in our latest LIC plans index.

Surrender, loan and the liquidity truth

After two full years of premiums the policy acquires a guaranteed surrender value, and a policy loan becomes available against it. Both facilities exist, and both are expensive in the early years: surrender values on traditional plans are computed from factors that deliberately front-load commission and risk charges, so a policy surrendered in years 3–5 typically returns only a fraction of premiums paid — often under half. The loan is the cheaper way to raise cash against a live policy: the cover continues, bonuses keep accruing, and repayment on schedule avoids unravelling 20 years of commitment. The discipline to take away: this is a 25-year ladder, not a savings account. Build the emergency fund first; treat surrender as a last resort with the arithmetic looked at in writing before you sign anything.

Tax treatment

Premiums qualify under Section 80C within the ₹1.5 lakh aggregate limit you share with EPF, PPF, ELSS and home-loan principal. Maturity proceeds and survival benefits fall under Section 10(10D) and stay tax-free provided the policy meets the statutory premium-to-sum-assured conditions — for policies issued from 1 April 2023 the annual-premium cap for the exemption (other than ULIPs) is ₹5 lakh, and Plan 921's premiums on retail sum assureds sit well inside it. The death benefit is unconditionally tax-free for the nominee. GST at 4.5% in year one and 2.25% thereafter is added to the net premium and is not recoverable — it is a real cost to net off when comparing against mutual-fund or FD alternatives.

What works

  • Fixed 15% payouts every five years — budgetable liquidity, performance-independent
  • Full 125% death cover survives every survival payout — family protection never dips
  • Participating bonuses on top of a guaranteed schedule; recent par declarations in the ₹46–48/1000 band
  • Premiums stop at year 20 while cover and bonuses run to year 25
  • Loan after two years — access liquidity without killing the contract

What does not

  • Withdrawn since 1 January 2025 — only existing holders can hold it
  • Early surrender values are punitive; years 3–5 returns a fraction of premiums
  • Interim payouts interrupt compounding — IRR lands mid-5%, not the 6–7% often quoted for LIC
  • 20-year premium commitment with lapse risk if cash flow breaks
  • Bonus and FAB rates are declared annually — only the sum assured is guaranteed

Is LIC New Money Back 25 Years (Plan 921) still available for purchase?

No. Plan 921 was withdrawn from new business on 1 January 2025. Existing policies continue on original terms — payouts, maturity, death cover and bonus participation are unaffected. New buyers looking for the same structure should examine the 20-year Plan 920 (20% payouts at years 5/10/15, 40% + bonuses at year 20), which we review in detail, or limited-premium Jeevan Labh 736.

Do the four survival payouts reduce the death cover?

No. The death benefit is defined on the basic sum assured — the higher of 125% of basic sum assured or 7 times the annualised premium, plus vested bonuses — and it does not step down after a survival payout. A policyholder who has collected all four 15% instalments still leaves the nominee entitled to 125% of the original sum assured.

What maturity amount should I expect on a ₹4 lakh sum assured?

The fixed components are ₹60,000 at each of years 5, 10, 15 and 20 (₹2,40,000 total) and ₹1,60,000 at year 25. Bonus is the variable: at recent participating declarations around ₹46–48 per ₹1,000 per year, indicative accrual is roughly ₹3.5–4.2 lakh over the term, plus an FAB if declared. Only the sum assured is guaranteed — treat bonus figures as planning bands, not promises.

What happens if I miss a premium?

A grace period applies from the due date as per the policy wording; if the premium stays unpaid the policy lapses. Standard LIC revival rules allow revival within two years of the first unpaid premium by paying arrears with interest (compounded half-yearly) plus evidence of continued insurability. Beyond two years the contract cannot be revived — cover and future bonuses are lost.

Should I surrender this policy early?

Rarely. Guaranteed surrender value only exists after two full premium years, and the factors are deliberately low in the early policy years — a year 3–5 surrender usually returns well under half of premiums paid. The policy loan (available from the same two-year point) raises cash while keeping cover and bonuses intact, and is almost always the better lever. Before surrendering, get the exact figure in writing and compare it against paying one more year.
