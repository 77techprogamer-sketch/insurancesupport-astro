Care Arogya Sanjeevani Review 2026: IRDAI Standard Policy, 5% Co-pay & Sub-limits

The standardized floor every insurer sells — arithmetic on the caps that decide your payout, not the premium.

Care Arogya Sanjeevani is the IRDAI-mandated standard health policy — every insurer's version carries the same core contract: ₹5–10 lakh sum insured, entry from 3 months to 65 years, lifelong renewal, a compulsory 5% co-pay on every claim, room and ICU caps tied to a percentage of sum assured, a ₹40,000-per-eye cataract sub-limit, and a claim-free bonus ladder of 5% per year capped at 50%. Because the wording is standardised, Care's version competes on price, service and network — and at its price point it is the cheapest compliant floor in Indian health insurance. This review works the caps with real arithmetic (they decide your payout far more than the premium does), maps what the standard product deliberately excludes, and positions it against Care Supreme and super top-ups.

Read the caps twice. On a ₹5 lakh policy the room limit is ₹5,000/day (the 2% formula hits its ceiling immediately) and ICU ₹10,000/day; on ₹10 lakh the same caps double to ₹10,000 and ₹20,000. A private-room family in a metro will exceed ₹5,000/day without trying — and once a room cap exists, the proportionate-deduction clause can reach ancillary charges billed with the room (consultant fees, nursing), not just the room itself.

The claim arithmetic: co-pay and caps on a real bill

Illustrative — actual settlement depends on room category, procedure limits and the final admissible bill. Save the settlement letter: it shows every deduction line by line.

Work the numbers: an admissible ₹4,75,000 after procedure checks → proportionate deduction if the room breached the cap (a 4-day stay at ₹7,500/day room on a 5L plan costs roughly ₹10,000–20,000 once linked charges are prorated) → 5% co-pay on the remainder → you receive in the high ₹4-lakh range on a ₹5-lakh bill. The pattern across claim data is consistent: Sanjeevani denials are rarely "the whole claim", they are stacked deductions that surprise policyholders who expected 95% of the sticker bill. Two controls: choose a room at or below the cap when the plan is the only cover, and layer a super top-up so a genuinely major event escalates to an uncapped product above ₹5–10 lakh. Cumulative bonus does the opposite job — every claim-free year your SI grows 5% to a maximum 50% (₹7.5 lakh on a 5L base), and under the standard wording that bonus is not clawed back because of a claim in a later year, which is more generous than private-variant no-claim ladders that reset.

What the standard product deliberately does not cover

The standardisation cuts both ways: exclusions are standardized too. Maternity and newborn expenses are not covered. Obesity treatment, cosmetic and experimental procedures, dental treatment except from accident, self-inflicted injuries, war and nuclear perils follow the standard exclusion list. Sub-limits apply to listed procedures — the cataract cap being the most-used one, where metro cataract packages of ₹45,000–60,000 per eye leave ₹5,000–20,000 per eye with you after the ₹40,000 sub-limit (and the 2-year wait means a cataract diagnosed in year one waits). Pre-existing conditions carry the schedule's multi-year wait — declare them honestly; the standard wording's contestability rules still apply. None of this makes the product bad — it makes it precise: Sanjeevani is a defined-benefit-ish floor, not a comprehensive family cover. You buy it knowing exactly which gaps you are accepting.

The decision logic: if the premium gap to an uncapped comprehensive plan is under roughly ₹10,000 a year for your age band, buy the comprehensive product — caps and sub-limits cost more than the delta over a decade. If budget is genuinely tight (young family, first policy, cash-flow pressure), Sanjeevani as the floor plus a super top-up above ₹5 lakh is the structurally smart answer: catastrophic risk moves to the top-up, the floor carries the routine claim with its known deductions, and total spend stays controlled. On Care's own shelf, Supreme sits above Sanjeevani (no sub-limits, no compulsory co-pay, restoration, larger SI options); Senior variants and Freedom handle the 60+ segment with their own trade-offs. Whatever you choose, claim workflows are shared: cashless pre-authorisation under IRDAI's 2026 pre-authorisation rules, reimbursement if the hospital is out of network, and the full objection-to-escalation path in our appeal guide if a settlement comes back short. Three claim scenarios, worked to the rupee

Scenario 1 — routine appendectomy, ₹1,80,000 bill (5L SI, claim-free year 3, bonus +15% → SI ₹5.75L). No procedure sub-limit applies. Admissible: ₹1,80,000 minus any non-admissible items (say ₹4,000 in consumables not on the schedule) → ₹1,76,000. Room: 4 days at ₹4,500 — under the ₹5,750 effective cap (2% of the bonus-adjusted SI), so no proportionate deduction. Co-pay 5% → ₹8,800. Payout: ₹1,67,200; you bear ₹12,800 including the non-admissible items. Scenario 2 — cardiac angioplasty with stent, ₹4,60,000 bill (5L SI, year 1, no bonus). Cardiac procedure limits under the standard schedule apply to the stent package — assume ₹2,00,000 admissible under that cap plus ₹2,20,000 other admissible components → ₹4,20,000; co-pay 5% → ₹21,000; room 6 days at ₹6,000 breaches the ₹5,000 cap by ₹1,000/day, prorating linked charges roughly ₹8,000–12,000 additional deduction. Net payout lands around ₹3,90,000 — 85% of the bill, not 95%, and the gap is entirely caps and co-pay working together. Scenario 3 — bilateral cataract in year 2 (₹55,000/eye metro package). Sub-limit ₹40,000/eye (2-year wait met at renewal) → ₹80,000 admissible cap on ₹1,10,000 actual; 5% co-pay → ₹4,000; payout ₹76,000, you bear ₹34,000. The lesson across all three: the standard product pays promptly and predictably, but "the bill" is never the number — run your city's typical procedure through the caps before deciding 5L Sanjeevani is enough on its own.

At purchase: what to verify before you pay

Three line-items decide whether the standard contract behaves as modeled: the co-pay % printed in the schedule (standard is 5%, but floater compositions and some distribution channels have attached small variations — if it reads 10%, the scenario math doubles against you), the procedure sub-limit annexure (cataract and listed surgeries), and the premium-revision note (age-banded increases and GST at 18%). Buy with the schedule PDF downloaded and stored, not just the brochure; the schedule is the contract in a dispute. Add the health declaration copy and payment receipt to the same folder — three documents that make any later claim or portability conversation start with facts instead of reconstruction. Porting at renewal (with waiting-period credit) and mid-term upgrades both work off this folder.

Strengths

  • IRDAI-standard wording — identical core contract across insurers, so price/service decide
  • ₹500–600/month-class premiums for a family floor; 5% co-pay caps insurer's own exposure too
  • Cumulative bonus to +50% without claim-clawback; lifelong renewal; wide cashless network
  • Perfect underlayer for a super top-up strategy

Weaknesses

  • Room and ICU caps trigger proportionate deductions across linked charges
  • Mandatory 5% co-pay and ₹40k cataract sub-limit bite on common procedures
  • No maternity, no restoration benefit — the 5L is a floor, not a family safety net
  • Only two SI options (5L/10L) — above that you need another product anyway

Who should buy it (and who should not)

Buy: first-time buyers establishing a compliant floor; young families stacking a super top-up above it; parents already covered elsewhere who need a cheap second layer; anyone whose budget cannot yet stretch to an uncapped comprehensive plan but who refuses to be uninsured. Skip: households needing more than ₹5–10 lakh as the primary cover (a maternity-track couple, a metro family with a hospital preference), buyers who will bristle at the ₹5,000 room cap on a routine claim, and anyone who can afford the comprehensive product at a sub-₹10k annual delta. Whatever the choice, revisit at renewal: cumulative bonus compounds the floor's usefulness, tariffs adjust yearly, and a stack that was right at 30 may be wrong at 45 — the senior-cover buying guide covers the later-life transition when this policy's renewal economics change fastest.

Is Care Arogya Sanjeevani the same at every insurer?

The core contract — SI options, 5% co-pay, room/ICU formulas, cataract sub-limit, cumulative bonus — is IRDAI-standardised. Price, network, claim service and renewal experience are where Care and its competitors differ, so compare those, not the wording.

What is the real cost of the room rent cap?

On a 5L policy the cap is ₹5,000/day. Exceeding it can trigger proportionate deduction of linked charges (nursing, consultation) across the whole bill — a ₹4.75 lakh admissible bill can net 10–20% below the headline after cap and co-pay math. Choose a room at or below the cap, or layer a super top-up.

Does the cumulative bonus get taken away after a claim?

Under the standard wording the claim-free bonus builds 5% per year up to 50% and is not reduced because you later claim — unlike no-claim ladders in some private variants that reset. Your settlement letter will show the bonus-adjusted SI in force for that claim.

Can I upgrade from Arogya Sanjeevani to a bigger Care plan later?

Yes — portability/upgrade with credit for completed waiting periods is permitted at renewal. Cumulative bonus history generally carries subject to underwriting; keep renewal receipts and the claim-free record to support the move at underwriting.

Does Care Arogya Sanjeevani include a restoration benefit?

No — the standard wording has no sum-insured restoration. Once a 5L claim exhausts the SI for that year, cover stops until renewal or cumulative bonus. This is the main structural reason to pair the base with a super top-up, which restores (or is itself untouched) above the base limit.
