Why Your Health Insurance Claim Was Rejected in 2026

Published: August 14, 2026 · By Hari Kotian · IRDAI-Certified Insurance Advisor, Bengaluru

Health insurance claims in India are being rejected at a higher rate than ever in 2026 — and most families don't find out until the hospital bills are on their table. The frustrating part? In many cases, the rejection could have been avoided entirely with the right documentation.

After resolving over 500 claim disputes in Karnataka, I've distilled the five most common reasons claims get denied, and the exact playbook we use to recover them.

1. Pre-Existing Disease Disclosure Gaps

The #1 reason claims are rejected. When you fill a proposal form, you list medications, diagnoses, and doctor visits. But the insurer interprets "diagnosis" broadly — even a doctor visit for a test can be counted as a pre-existing condition if you didn't disclose it.

Key fact: Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act 1938, after 2 years of continuous coverage, the insurer cannot repudiate a claim on grounds of non-disclosure — unless they can prove fraudulent intent. This is where many claims are wrongly rejected.

What to do: Get your doctor to issue a letter clarifying that the condition was first diagnosed AFTER the policy inception date, not before.

2. Waiting Period Claims (30-Day / 2-Year)

Every health plan has a waiting period: usually 30 days for the first claim, 2 years for specific diseases, and 4 years for PEDs. Claims filed before these windows close are almost always rejected — and this is actually correct.

What to do: Check your policy schedule for exact waiting period dates. If you're claiming within the first 30 days for a new hospitalization unrelated to a PED, some insurers still reject — this is wrong and needs to be escalated.

3. Documents Don't Match the Policy

We see this every week. The hospital's diagnosis code says one thing, the insured's proposal form says another. Insurers use this mismatch to reject.

Key documents to keep perfectly aligned:

  • Original proposal form (your copy)
  • Hospital discharge summary
  • All prescription receipts and pathology reports
  • Claim form (Form 34B for health claims)

4. Informal Treatment Outside the Network

Cashless claims from network hospitals are straightforward. But if you went to a non-network hospital, you need to file a reimbursement claim within 15–30 days. Many people miss this window because they assume the claim was "in progress" from the TPA.

5. The "Moral Hazard" Clause

This is the one insurers don't explain well. If the claim amount is disproportionately high relative to the premiums paid, the insurer may flag it as "moral hazard" and delay or reject. This is especially common with high-value health plans (₹10L+).

The 5-Step Recovery Playbook

Every claim we've successfully recovered used this same playbook — and you can follow it yourself:

  1. Read the rejection letter carefully — note the exact policy clause cited
  2. Pull the corresponding policy wording page — cross-reference with the claim decision
  3. Build a parallel evidence file — prescriptions, WhatsApp timestamps, pharmacy receipts
  4. File an Internal Feedback Mechanism (IFM) — every insurer has an internal appeals process; this is free and should be used first
  5. Lodge an IRDAI complaint — only after the IFM is rejected; attach the IFM decision letter + your 20-page document bundle
Average timeline: IFM: 15–30 days · IRDAI complaint: 30–60 days · Full recovery (after escalation): 60–90 days

Case Study: ₹18.5 Lakhs Recovered for a Family in Bengaluru

A 42-year-old client in Koramangala had his cancer treatment claim (₹18.5 lakhs) rejected — the insurer cited a pre-existing condition from a routine acidity medication mentioned in the original proposal form. The insurer argued this constituted non-disclosure of a gastric condition that "could have led to" the cancer diagnosis 3 years later.

Our response:

  • We obtained the hospital pathology report proving the cancer diagnosis was a new condition — the first formal diagnosis was 11 months after policy inception.
  • We filed the IFM within 30 days — the insurer upheld the rejection citing "moral hazard".
  • We escalated to IRDAI with a 27-page bundle including: original proposal form, all pharmacy records dated post-policy, and two independent oncologist letters.
  • Result: Full ₹18.5 lakhs paid within 21 days.

Should You Go Alone or Hire an Advisor?

If the rejection letter clearly cites a correct exclusion (e.g., you claimed within a waiting period), there's no basis for recovery. But in the majority of cases — particularly "pre-existing disease" and "non-disclosure" rejections — a professional review is almost always worth it.
