Why Insurers Reject Claims (and What You Can Do)

In 2026, a surprising number of insurance claims in India are getting rejected — not because the policy is invalid, but because of how the paperwork is filled or interpreted. Here are the main reasons we see claims denied, and how a proper advisor can help:

  • Delayed intimation — you didn’t inform the insurer within the time window specified in the policy.
  • Incomplete documentation — missing discharge summaries, police FIRs (in case of theft), or the original policy document.
  • Mismatched declarations — the health or income details you gave during purchase don’t match the hospital records.
  • Clawback clauses — for health/term plans held under 4 years, any material misstatement can lead to the insurer demanding you return the sum assured.
  • Waiting vs excluded conditions — a pre-existing condition might be excluded, or the waiting period hasn’t expired.

The Recovery Checklist That Actually Works

Every claim we’ve successfully recovered started with the same 5-step checklist. You can do this yourself, but the average family loses 2–4 weeks going back and forth with the insurer, which is exactly where things fall apart.

  1. Check the rejection letter — note the exact clause cited (e.g., UCA, §17(2), Section 45). This tells you the angle of attack.
  2. Review your policy wording — pull the exact page/paragraph of the benefit or exclusion clause the insurer is relying on.
  3. Gather contempororative evidence — WhatsApp timestamps, doctor prescriptions dated before the policy lapsed, the original hospital estimate, etc.
  4. File an Internal Feedback Mechanism (IFM) — most insurers have an internal claims review appellate authority. This is cheaper than IRDAI and faster.
  5. Approach the IRDAI grievance portal — only after exhausting the IFM, but usually within 14 days.

A Real Case: How We Recovered ₹18.5 Lakhs for a Family in Koramangala

Last year, a 42-year-old professional from Koramangala had his cancer treatment claim rejected by a top-tier insurer citing “pre-existing disease non-disclosure”. The original policy was a 5-year health plan; the claim was filed during the 3rd year. The treatment cost ₹18.5 lakhs. Here’s what we did:

  • We reviewed the original proposal form — the insured had mentioned occasional acidity medication but NOT a formal diagnosis. We demonstrated this was a new diagnosis, not a pre-existing condition, with the hospital’s pathology report dated 11 months after policy inception.
  • We filed the IFM within 30 days — the insurer upheld the rejection citing “moral hazard”.
  • We approached IRDAI with a 27-page document bundle — including the pharmacy prescription dates proving continuous medication post-policy.
  • Result: Full claim of ₹18.5 lakhs paid within 21 days of IRDAI escalation.
