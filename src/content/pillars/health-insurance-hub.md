## Health Insurance in India: A Practical Guide to Choosing Cover and Using It

Health insurance is a contract that pays eligible medical expenses under stated terms, limits, exclusions, and procedures. A policy is useful only when the cover matches the people insured, the hospitals they may use, and the costs they need protection from. This guide explains how Indian families can compare policies, prepare for a claim, and handle a dispute without relying on headline benefit names alone.

There is no single “best” sum insured or policy for every household. Age, existing illnesses, city, family structure, employer cover, budget, and access to savings all matter. Read the policy wording and customer information sheet for the exact product before buying; brochures and comparison tables are summaries.

### Understand the main forms of cover

| Cover type | How it works | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Individual policy | Each insured person has their own cover | Is a separate sum insured useful for someone with higher health needs? |
| Family floater | Covered family members share a sum insured | Could one person's large claim leave less cover for others in the same year? |
| Employer or group cover | Cover is arranged through an employer or group | What happens if employment ends, and can cover be continued or converted? |
| Top-up or super top-up | Adds cover after a specified deductible is met | How is the deductible calculated, and does it apply per claim or across the year? |
| Critical illness policy | Pays a defined benefit on a listed diagnosis if policy conditions are met | Which illnesses, definitions, survival period, and proof requirements apply? |

These products solve different problems. A fixed-benefit critical illness plan is not a substitute for a hospitalization policy, and a top-up does not work as expected unless you understand how its deductible interacts with your base cover.

### Decide what risk the policy must cover

Start with a household map: list each person, age, current cover, known conditions, preferred hospitals, and who depends on employer insurance. Then estimate how much of a major treatment bill you could pay without selling assets or borrowing. This is a planning exercise, not a prediction of future medical prices. Compare different coverage limits and premium levels against that budget.

For a family with children and older parents, one floater may not be the simplest choice. A large claim by one member can use a shared limit; an older member may also have different underwriting, co-payment, or waiting-period terms. Compare a separate senior policy with a family floater using the actual offers and exclusions, rather than assuming one structure is always cheaper or better.

### Compare the policy terms that change claim outcomes

#### Room eligibility and proportionate deductions

Check the eligible room category, any room-rent cap, and the effect the wording assigns to choosing a higher category. Ask the insurer for a worked example using the policy terms. A room limit can affect more than the room bill if the policy wording applies proportionate deductions to related expenses.

#### Co-payment, deductible, and sub-limits

A co-payment is the share of an admissible claim you must pay. A deductible is an amount that must be met before the policy pays, while a sub-limit caps a particular benefit or treatment. Review each one separately. A lower premium may come with a higher share of costs or narrower limits.

#### Waiting periods and pre-existing conditions

Look for the initial waiting period, condition-specific waiting periods, and the definition of a pre-existing disease in the contract. Disclose medical history accurately on the proposal form, including past consultations, tests, medicines, and diagnoses asked about. If you are unsure whether something is relevant, disclose it with context and keep a copy of what you submitted. Never rely on a verbal assurance that a condition “does not matter.”

IRDAI's health insurance materials describe a 60-month moratorium period for continuous coverage, including applicable portability or migration credits, after which a policy or claim is generally not contestable on non-disclosure or misrepresentation grounds except for established fraud. This is a rule with defined conditions, not a reason to omit information at purchase. Read the current [IRDAI Health Insurance guidance and circulars](https://irdai.gov.in/health-dept) and your policy wording.

#### Network hospitals and cashless access

Check whether your preferred hospitals are in the insurer's current network. Network lists can change. Cashless treatment is a payment arrangement between the insurer, hospital, and policyholder; it is not a guarantee that every expense will be admitted. The insurer may approve, query, or decline a request under the contract. Ask the hospital insurance desk to submit a complete pre-authorization and keep a copy of the response.

#### Restoration, no-claim bonus, and exclusions

“Restore,” “reload,” and “bonus” benefits have conditions. Check when they activate, whether they apply to the same illness or same insured person, and whether unused benefits expire at renewal. Read the exclusions and definitions rather than relying on a product name. Treatment described as modern, day care, domiciliary, or consumable may have specific limits or conditions.

### Read the policy before completing the purchase

Before paying, compare the proposal answers with what you actually disclosed. Confirm names, dates of birth, nominees if applicable, sum insured, selected options, waiting periods, exclusions, co-payment, deductible, and policy period. Save the proposal form, premium receipt, customer information sheet, full wording, and policy schedule. Once the policy arrives, compare the issued document with the offer and request correction of any discrepancy through the insurer's official service channel.

If a free-look period applies to your policy, use the period and process stated in the policy and current rules. It is designed to review the contract after receipt; it is not a substitute for checking terms before purchase. Keep proof of the date the policy was received and any cancellation request.

### How to use health insurance during treatment

#### Planned admission

Call the insurer or TPA using the details on the policy card and confirm the current process. Ask the hospital to submit pre-authorization with the diagnosis, proposed treatment, and estimate. Provide accurate information and respond promptly to document queries. Keep the request number and written approval or denial. If the requested amount is approved only in part, ask what expenses remain outside the authorization and why.

#### Emergency admission

Prioritize medical care. Notify the insurer as soon as practical and follow the policy's notification requirements. If cashless authorization is unavailable or not approved, ask the hospital for records and invoices needed to submit a reimbursement claim, if the policy allows it. A cashless decision at the hospital stage does not by itself determine whether a later reimbursement claim is payable.

#### Reimbursement claim

Submit the claim through the insurer's current channel and follow the policy's stated requirements and deadlines. Keep a copy of the complete file, including itemized bills, receipts, prescriptions, diagnostic reports, discharge summary, and any forms. If originals are requested, scan them first and keep a dispatch record. Do not send unrelated documents simply to make the bundle larger; include an index and answer each requirement clearly.

### If a health claim is queried, reduced, or denied

First distinguish a pending file from a final rejection. Request the insurer's written status, the exact missing items, or the reason and policy clause for a reduction or denial. Compare the decision with the schedule and wording. For a partial settlement, ask for an itemized calculation. For a medical issue, obtain a treating doctor's note that addresses the specific question; a general certificate may not answer the insurer's concern.

Send a formal grievance through the insurer's process with the claim number, a short chronology, the clause you rely on, the evidence, and the remedy requested. Keep the grievance reference. If unresolved, the [IRDAI Bima Bharosa portal](https://bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in/) can register and track a grievance. Eligible disputes may also be considered through the [Insurance Ombudsman](https://www.cioins.co.in/), subject to its current rules and jurisdiction. See our [health claim appeal guide](/blog/health-insurance-claim-rejected-appeal-guide-2026/) for a focused checklist.

### Portability and changing insurers

Portability may allow eligible credits earned under existing cover—such as applicable waiting-period credits—to transfer to a new insurer, subject to the current rules, underwriting, product terms, and the information exchanged. Do not cancel existing cover before a new policy is accepted and its terms are clear. Submit the request within the process and window set by the insurers and current IRDAI rules. Ask in writing which credits are recognized, what new terms apply, and when the new cover begins.

Compare the new policy's exclusions, co-payments, room limits, network hospitals, claim process, and premium at renewal, not just the first-year price. For a step-by-step look at rejected or delayed transfer requests, see the [health insurance portability guide](/blog/health-insurance-portability-india-2026-switch-insurer-without-losing-benefits/).

### Coordinate employer and personal health cover

Employer cover can be valuable, but its terms, covered family members, sum insured, and continuation options are set by the group arrangement. Do not assume it will remain available after a job change or retirement. A personal policy can provide continuity independent of employment, while an employer policy may add cover during the current job. If you have both, tell each insurer about the other cover when asked and follow each policy's coordination process when making a claim. Keep separate schedules and claim references so an employer change does not leave the household unsure which policy is active.

When considering a personal policy after joining a new employer, compare the waiting-period credits and underwriting terms in writing. A group policy may have different entry rules from an individual retail policy. If you leave employment, ask the group administrator and insurer promptly whether migration or another continuation option applies and what deadlines and terms govern it. Do not assume portability or migration happens automatically.

### Compare annual cost over more than one renewal

The first-year premium is only one part of the decision. Compare the premium at current age, renewal conditions, co-payment or deductible, room limits, waiting periods, and the cost of adding family members. Check whether a discounted price is conditional on a higher deductible or a particular payment method. Ask how a claim can affect a no-claim bonus or renewal benefit under the exact wording. A cheaper policy can be suitable, but compare the amount of risk the household would retain if a large claim occurred.

### A practical annual review checklist

- Confirm every insured person's name, age, contact details, and coverage limit.
- Recheck preferred hospitals and the insurer's current network list.
- Review waiting periods, exclusions, co-payments, deductibles, and sub-limits.
- Update the insurer about changes requested by the policy's service process.
- Save renewal receipts and the latest schedule and wording.
- Review whether employer cover is portable, continued, or lost when employment changes.
- Compare a top-up only after checking the deductible and claim coordination.
- Keep the insurer's grievance contacts and policy service channels accessible.

## FAQ

**Q: How much health insurance should a family buy?**
There is no fixed amount that suits every family. Consider city, ages, medical history, household budget, existing cover, employer benefits, and the amount you could pay yourself for a large bill. Compare base cover and a super top-up only after understanding the deductible.

**Q: Does cashless approval guarantee that the insurer will pay the whole hospital bill?**
No. Cashless authorization is subject to policy terms and may cover only eligible expenses or an approved amount. Keep the final bill, records, and written authorization and review any deductions at settlement.

**Q: Should I disclose a past illness if it is cured?**
Answer every proposal question fully and accurately. Disclose relevant history with dates and context, even if treatment ended, and keep a copy of the submitted answers. The insurer assesses it under the proposal and policy terms.

**Q: Can I switch health insurers without losing waiting-period credit?**
Portability rules provide for transfer of eligible credits, subject to the current process and terms. Get the new insurer's acceptance and written confirmation of recognized credits before cancelling the existing policy.

**Q: Is a rejected cashless request the same as a rejected reimbursement claim?**
Not necessarily. A cashless request may not be approved at that time, while a later reimbursement claim can be assessed separately under the policy. Obtain the written reason and follow the policy's claim instructions.

**Q: What should I do if a health claim is partly paid?**
Ask for an itemized settlement statement, identify each disputed deduction, and compare it with the policy wording and medical records. Send a written grievance with evidence against each item.
