## Insurance Claim Recovery in India: A Policyholder's Complete Guide

An insurer's decision should be assessed against the policy wording, the facts of the loss, and the documents in the claim file. A claim marked “pending,” reduced, or rejected is not automatically correct or incorrect; the next step is to identify the exact reason and test it against the contract and evidence. This guide explains how to do that for health, life, motor, and other insurance claims in India.

The process below is a practical guide, not a promise that every disputed claim will be paid. The insurer decides claims under the policy and applicable rules. A representative can help organize and present a case, but cannot guarantee an outcome or replace legal advice where a court or tribunal issue arises.

### Start with the claim status, not assumptions

People use “claim rejected” to describe several different outcomes. A claim may be awaiting documents, under investigation, approved for less than the amount claimed, denied cashless authorization, or formally repudiated. These situations call for different responses. Ask the insurer to confirm the current status in writing and provide the specific policy condition or document requirement behind it.

| Status | What it usually means | First practical step |
|---|---|---|
| Pending or query raised | A decision has not yet been made, or the insurer needs information | Ask for a complete list of requirements and the claim reference number |
| Cashless authorization not approved | The hospital payment arrangement was not approved at that stage | Obtain the written reason; ask the hospital about reimbursement submission under the policy |
| Partly settled | The insurer accepted some items and disallowed or reduced others | Request an itemized calculation and clause-by-clause basis for each deduction |
| Rejected or repudiated | The insurer says the claim is not payable under the contract | Get the final written decision, cited clause, and grievance route |

### The claim process: a repeatable sequence

#### 1. Notify the insurer and protect the evidence

Use the insurer's official website, app, policy card, or listed customer service channel to notify it. Save the date, claim number, and confirmation. Follow the policy's notification instructions as soon as reasonably possible. If an emergency or practical barrier delayed notice, explain it truthfully and keep evidence of the circumstances. Do not alter bills, reports, photographs, or dates to make a file appear stronger.

For motor losses, preserve photographs, repair estimates, police records where relevant, and communication with the surveyor. For health claims, keep medical records and itemized bills. For a life claim, secure the policy details, death certificate, and claimant identity documents. Requirements differ by policy and type of event, so the policy schedule and wording remain the controlling checklist.

#### 2. Build one complete claim file

Create a dated folder containing the policy schedule, full policy wording, proposal form if available, endorsements, premium receipts, claim form, insurer correspondence, and every document submitted. Keep copies of originals before handing anything over. Record the submission channel and date for each document. If a portal accepts only some files, save screenshots or confirmation emails and follow up for the missing items.

The proposal form matters because disputes can turn on what was asked and what was disclosed when cover was purchased. Do not rely only on a summary, brochure, agent's message, or online comparison page. If the proposal form is unavailable, request a copy from the insurer.

#### 3. Ask for the exact reason and calculation

If the insurer declines, delays, or reduces a claim, ask for a reasoned written response that identifies the relevant policy term and the facts relied upon. For a partial settlement, request an itemized calculation showing each amount allowed or deducted. For a pending claim, ask what remains outstanding and whether the request is complete. A phone explanation can be useful, but follow it with a written record.

#### 4. Match each reason to the contract and evidence

Read the cited clause in context. Check the definitions, exclusions, waiting periods, limits, deductibles, sub-limits, conditions, and endorsements that apply. Then compare those terms with the actual records. A useful review has four columns: insurer's reason, contract clause, relevant fact, and supporting evidence. This exposes whether the issue is a missing document, a factual disagreement, a policy interpretation, or a calculation.

For example, if a health claim is reduced for a room-related limit, compare the selected room category, the policy's room eligibility, and the insurer's calculation of linked deductions. If a motor repair is reduced for depreciation, compare the applicable schedule, add-ons, vehicle age, and estimate. These are examples of questions to investigate, not conclusions about a particular policy.

#### 5. Send a focused grievance to the insurer

Use the insurer's current grievance redressal process and address the complaint to its Grievance Redressal Officer when appropriate. Include the policy number, claim number, dates, the decision being challenged, the clause you rely on, the evidence, and the exact remedy requested. Keep the tone factual. Attach an index of documents and request acknowledgement with a grievance reference number.

If a requirement is genuinely missing, provide it and explain what it is intended to establish. If it is unavailable, say why and ask whether alternative evidence can be considered. Do not send repeated unindexed bundles that make it harder to see the issue. Keep every response and note deadlines stated in the policy, insurer communication, or applicable rules.

#### 6. Escalate through the appropriate external route

If the insurer's response does not resolve the issue, you can use IRDAI's [Bima Bharosa grievance portal](https://bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in/) to register or track a grievance. It is a regulatory grievance channel; it is not an automatic order directing payment. Keep the insurer's complaint reference and supporting documents available.

The [Insurance Ombudsman Rules](https://www.cioins.co.in/notification/Insurance%20Ombudsman%20Rules%2C%202017%28%20as%20amended%20till%2009.11.2023%29.pdf) provide a separate complaint route for eligible individual policyholders and claimants. The rules set conditions, including first making a written representation to the insurer and circumstances in which the insurer rejects the complaint, gives an unsatisfactory reply, or does not reply within one month. The amended rules set an award ceiling of ₹50 lakh including relevant expenses, subject to the rule's terms. Check the [Council for Insurance Ombudsmen](https://www.cioins.co.in/) for current jurisdiction, filing instructions, and eligibility before submitting. A complaint generally cannot be taken to the Ombudsman if the same subject is already pending before or decided by a court, consumer forum, or arbitrator; verify the current rules for your situation.

#### 7. Consider specialist or legal help when the issue warrants it

Independent review can be useful where the loss is substantial, the contract language is disputed, the insurer relies on alleged non-disclosure or fraud, multiple policies interact, or the claimant is unsure which forum has jurisdiction. Ask any adviser to explain their role, fees, conflicts, and what they can and cannot do. Do not hand over original documents or account passwords without a clear and safe reason. For legal limitation periods or litigation strategy, speak with a qualified lawyer.

### Documents that commonly help

The right file depends on the policy. These are common starting points, not a universal mandatory list:

- **All claims:** policy schedule and wording, claim form, claim reference, decision or query letter, correspondence, proof of premium payment, identity and bank details requested by the insurer.
- **Health:** admission and discharge records, treating doctor's notes, investigation reports, prescriptions, itemized bills, receipts, pharmacy bills, and pre-authorization communication where relevant.
- **Life:** policy details, death certificate, claimant and nominee documents, medical or accident records requested for the event, and bank details. The insurer may ask for additional items based on the policy and circumstances.
- **Motor:** policy and registration details, driving licence where relevant, photographs, repair estimate, surveyor communication, invoices, and police documents when required by the event or policy.

When sending sensitive records, use the insurer's official channel. Redact unrelated personal information from documents where it is safe and permitted to do so.

### Common reasons files stall—and how to respond

**The request is vague.** Ask the insurer to list each missing item and explain what period or issue it relates to. Submit a numbered response against that list.

**The decision quotes a clause without explaining the facts.** Ask how the clause applies to the specific treatment, event, date, vehicle part, or policy declaration in your file.

**The amount is lower than expected.** Request the calculation and compare it with the schedule, limits, deductibles, co-payments, depreciation terms, and applicable add-ons.

**Different teams give different answers.** Keep communications in writing, quote the same claim reference, and request one consolidated response from the grievance team.

**A third-party administrator or hospital desk gave a verbal refusal.** Ask the insurer for its own written decision and the applicable grievance process. A desk-level communication may not be the final claim decision.

### A simple grievance letter structure

1. Identify the policyholder, policy, claim, and insurer decision.
2. State the outcome requested: reconsideration, itemized recalculation, a written decision, or review of a specific document.
3. Quote the relevant policy wording accurately and explain how the facts fit it.
4. List evidence by filename or page number; avoid unsupported accusations.
5. Ask the insurer to acknowledge the complaint and provide its grievance reference.

Keep the original message and proof of delivery. If you send additional documents later, label them as supplements to the same grievance.

Before sending, make the requested remedy measurable. For an unpaid claim, ask for reconsideration and settlement of the admissible amount under the cited clause. For a reduction, identify the disputed line items and request a revised calculation. For a stalled claim, request a status, the full outstanding-document list, and the decision date or next action the insurer can confirm. This helps the grievance team respond to the actual problem instead of treating the message as a general complaint.

Maintain a simple chronology with four columns: date, event, person or channel, and evidence. Include the date of hospital admission or loss, notification, each upload, each insurer query, your response, and the final decision. A chronology is especially useful when a dispute concerns late notice, whether a document was already submitted, or how long the insurer has held the file. It also helps an adviser or lawyer understand the matter without relying on memory.

## FAQ

**Q: What should I do first after an insurance claim is rejected?**
Request the final written decision, the exact policy clause, and the grievance process. Then compare the reason with your policy wording and evidence before sending a concise written grievance to the insurer.

**Q: Can I complain to IRDAI or the Insurance Ombudsman directly?**
You can register a grievance through IRDAI's Bima Bharosa channel. Ombudsman complaints have separate eligibility and procedural rules, including first approaching the insurer. Check the current CIO Rules and guidance before filing.

**Q: Does a claim rejection mean the insurer acted illegally?**
No. A rejection may be supported by the contract and facts, or it may be open to review. The written reason, policy wording, and complete claim file are needed to assess it.

**Q: Can an insurance adviser guarantee claim recovery?**
No responsible adviser can guarantee an insurer, Ombudsman, or court outcome. An adviser may help organize evidence, interpret the documents, and present a grievance; the decision remains with the relevant authority.

**Q: How long does a claim appeal take?**
There is no single timeline for every claim and route. Timing depends on the insurer's process, missing documents, investigation, and the forum involved. Follow the deadlines in current rules and written notices, and keep proof of each submission.

**Q: What if I disagree with a partial settlement?**
Ask for an itemized calculation and challenge each disputed deduction against the policy schedule, wording, and supporting records. You can use the insurer's grievance process for the unresolved amount.
