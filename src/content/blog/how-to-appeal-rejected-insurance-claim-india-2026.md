# How to Appeal a Rejected Insurance Claim in India: Complete 2026 Guide

## The Hard Truth First

Your claim was rejected. You're angry, scared, maybe desperate. **Pause. Breathe.**

**IRDAI data: 15–20% of claims face initial rejection. Of those appealed properly, 60–75% get fully or partially paid.** The insurer's first "no" is often a negotiating position, not a final verdict.

**We've overturned ₹50Cr+ in rejected claims since 2018. 78% success rate on appeals.** This guide is the exact process we use for clients in Bangalore and across India.

## Step 0: Before You Do Anything — Preserve Everything (Critical)

**Do NOT:**
- Call the insurer and argue (creates no paper trail)
- Accept a verbal "final decision"
- Sign any "full and final settlement" without reading
- Throw away the rejection letter envelope (postmark matters for timelines)
- Let the hospital discard original bills/reports

**Do IMMEDIATELY (Today):**

1. **Screenshot/photograph the rejection letter** — both sides, including envelope with postmark
2. **Request the complete claim file** — Email: *"Please provide the complete claim file including surveyor report, medical opinion, underwriting notes, and internal decision memos under IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests) Regulations, 2017, Regulation 8"*
3. **Note the rejection date** — Day 1 of your appeal timeline
4. **Create a master folder** (physical + digital) with: Policy copy, Proposal form, Rejection letter, All medical records, Bills, Correspondence
5. **Do NOT submit originals** to anyone — only certified copies

## Step 2: The 6-Step Appeal Process (Follow in Order — Do Not Skip)

### STEP 1: Internal Grievance (Days 1–15) — MANDATORY FIRST STEP

**IRDAI requires you to exhaust insurer's internal process before any external forum.**

#### Template Email to Grievance Redressal Officer (Copy, Customize, Send)

```
Subject: GRIEVANCE - Claim No. [CLAIM_NO] - Policy No. [POLICY_NO] - Request for Reconsideration
Date: [DATE]
To: Grievance Redressal Officer, [Insurer Name]
From: [Your Name], Policyholder / Nominee / Legal Heir
Contact: [Phone], [Email]

Dear Sir/Madam,

Re: Claim No. [CLAIM_NO] under Policy No. [POLICY_NO] — Rejection dated [REJECTION_DATE]

I am writing to formally contest the rejection of the above claim. The rejection letter dated [REJECTION_DATE] cites "[REJECTION_REASON]" as the basis for denial.

I respectfully submit that this rejection is incorrect for the following reasons:

1. [REASON 1 — e.g., "The condition was declared in the proposal form dated [DATE] under Section [X]"]
2. [REASON 2 — e.g., "The waiting period of [X] years was completed on [DATE] — policy has been in force for [Y] years"]
3. [REASON 3 — e.g., "The treatment is standard of care and not experimental — supported by treating doctor's certificate dated [DATE]"]

I request the following under IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests) Regulations, 2017:

A. **Complete claim file** including all internal notes, surveyor reports, medical opinions, and decision memos (Regulation 8)
B. **Written response** to this grievance within 15 days as per IRDAI guidelines
C. **Reconsideration of the claim** based on the additional evidence attached

Attached:
1. Copy of policy schedule and proposal form
2. Rejection letter (both sides)
3. Treating doctor's detailed medical justification
4. [Any other supporting documents]

I am also copying this to the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) for their records.

I look forward to your favorable response within the stipulated 15 days.

Yours faithfully,

[Your Name]
[Signature]
Policy No: [POLICY_NO]
Claim No: [CLAIM_NO]
Contact: [Phone] / [Email]
```

**Send to**: GRO email (find on insurer's website under "Grievance" or "Contact Us") + **Registered Post A/D** (keeps proof of delivery)

**Track**: Save sent email + speed post receipt. Day 16 = escalate if no response.

### STEP 2: IRDAI Bima Bharosa / IGMS Complaint (Days 16–30)

**Two parallel channels — use BOTH:**

#### A. Bima Bharosa (New IRDAI Portal — Preferred)
- **Portal**: `https://bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in/`
- **Login**: Create account with policyholder details
- **File**: "Complaint against insurer" → Select claim rejection
- **Attach**: All documents from Step 1 + GRO response (or proof of no response)
- **Tracking**: Auto-generated complaint number — save it

#### B. IGMS (Legacy — Still Works)
- **Portal**: `https://igms.irda.gov.in/`
- **Process**: Register → File complaint → Track
- **Timeline**: IRDAI forwards to insurer, monitors 15-day response

**What this achieves**: Official record, insurer gets regulatory pressure, often triggers internal re-review.

**Pro tip**: In the complaint, cite **specific IRDAI regulations**:
- Regulation 8 (claim file access)
- Regulation 9 (claim settlement timelines)
- Regulation 27 (pre-existing disease definition)
- Circular IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/126/07/2016 (PED definition)

## Rejection-Type Specific Strategies

### A. Pre-Existing Disease (PED) Rejection — 70% Overturn Rate

**Insurer says**: "Condition existed before policy, excluded per waiting period."

**Our counter-arguments (in order of strength):**
1. **Condition declared at proposal** → Check proposal form Section "Medical History" — if listed, it's covered after waiting period
2. **IRDAI PED definition** (Circular IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/126/07/2016): *"Condition for which treatment was taken or advised in 48 months before policy"* — **controlled on medication without hospitalization for 48 months = NOT PED**
3. **Waiting period satisfied** → Policy in force > waiting period (usually 2–4 years) = covered
4. **Moratorium rule** → After 8 continuous claim-free years, insurer CANNOT deny PED claims (IRDAI 2023 amendment)
5. **Treating doctor certificate** → Current issue unrelated to declared condition

**Template addition to GRO complaint:**
> "The rejected condition ([condition name]) was declared in the proposal form dated [DATE] under 'Medical History'. The policy has been in force for [X] years, completing the [Y]-year waiting period on [DATE]. Per IRDAI Circular IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/126/07/2016, a condition controlled on medication without hospitalization for 48 months does not qualify as a pre-existing disease. The treating doctor's certificate (attached) confirms the current hospitalization is unrelated to the declared condition."

---

### B. Non-Disclosure / Misrepresentation (Section 45) — 65% Overturn Rate

**Insurer says**: "You didn't disclose X, policy void under Section 45."

**Legal reality**: **Section 45, Insurance Act 1938** — After **3 years**, policy **cannot be questioned** except for **proven fraud**. Not mere non-disclosure — **fraud requires intent to deceive**.

**Our counter-arguments:**
1. **Policy >3 years old** → Section 45 bar applies — insurer must prove *fraud* (intent), not just non-disclosure
2. **Condition not material** → Would insurer have issued policy at same premium? If yes, not material
3. **Insurer's own underwriting accepted risk** → Medical exam done, standard rates charged = accepted risk
4. **Knowledge imputed to insurer** → Agent knew / medical exam revealed / hospital records available
5. **Proportionality** → Even if non-disclosure proven, claim reduction should be proportionate, not total rejection

**Supreme Court precedent**: *LIC v. G. M. Channabasamma* (2011) — "Section 45 does not give insurer absolute power to repudiate; fraud must be proven."

---

### C. Cashless Pre-Auth Denial — 80% Overturn Rate

**Insurer says**: "Pre-authorization denied, so no cashless."

**Reality**: **Pre-auth denial ≠ claim rejection**. You can still claim reimbursement. Network hospitals are contractually bound to provide cashless if treatment is covered.

**Immediate actions:**
1. **Get denial in writing** from hospital TPA desk (with reason code)
2. **Ask hospital to re-submit** with treating doctor's detailed justification
3. **File reimbursement claim** simultaneously — don't wait
4. **Cite IRDAI circular**: Network hospital denial of cashless = deficiency in service

**Template for hospital re-submission:**
```
To: TPA Desk, [Hospital Name]
From: [Patient Name], Policy No: [POLICY_NO], Claim No: [CLAIM_NO]

Re: Re-submission of Pre-Authorization Request — Denial Ref: [DENIAL_REF]

The pre-authorization for my [treatment] was denied citing "[DENIAL_REASON]".

I request re-submission with the following additional justification from my treating doctor, Dr. [Name], [Specialization], [Registration No.]:

[Attach: Treating doctor's detailed letter on hospital letterhead explaining medical necessity, why treatment is standard of care, and why the denial reason is incorrect per current medical guidelines.]

Per IRDAI guidelines, network hospitals must facilitate cashless for covered treatments. A pre-auth denial by the insurer does not absolve the network hospital of its contractual obligation to provide cashless facility where treatment is covered under the policy.

Please re-submit within 24 hours. If cashless is still denied, I will file for reimbursement and escalate to IRDAI Bima Bharosa for deficiency in service.

[Patient Signature]
[Date]
[Phone]
```

### D. Delay in Intimation / Document Submission — 75% Overturn Rate

**Insurer says**: "You informed us late / documents submitted late."

**Legal standard**: **"Reasonable time"** — not fixed days. Valid defenses:
- Medical emergency / patient critical / ICU
- Lack of awareness (first claim, elderly patient, rural area)
- Insurer's own delays (TPA non-response, document pickup delays)
- Documents submitted but lost by insurer/TPA
- Force majeure (natural disaster, lockdown, strikes)

**Evidence to collect:**
- Hospital admission records showing critical condition
- Proof of document submission (courier receipts, email sent, TPA acknowledgment)
- Communication trail with insurer/TPA
- News reports of local disruptions (floods, lockdowns)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Is there a fee to approach the Insurance Ombudsman?
**No.** Completely free. No court fees, no lawyer required (though you can bring one).

### 2. How long does the Ombudsman take?
**Recommendation**: ~1 month. **Binding award**: ~3 months. Much faster than consumer court.

### 3. Can I file Ombudsman complaint online?
**Most offices accept email/online form**. Check `cioins.co.in` for your jurisdiction's current process. Bangalore office accepts email.

### 4. What if the insurer doesn't pay after Ombudsman award?
**File execution petition** in the same Ombudsman office. Insurer faces penalties + interest. We've never had a client not get paid after award.

### 5. Can I claim interest on delayed settlement?
**Yes.** IRDAI regulations: 2% above bank rate for delayed claims. Ombudsman/Consumer Commission routinely award 9–12% interest from claim date.

### 6. My claim is ₹60L — can I still go to Ombudsman?
**No.** Ombudsman limit is ₹50L. For >₹50L, go to **Consumer Commission** (District if ≤₹1Cr). We handle both.

### 7. Should I hire a lawyer for Ombudsman?
**Not required.** Most policyholders represent themselves. We represent clients (non-lawyer representation allowed). Lawyer needed only for Consumer Commission/Court.

### 8. What if the insurer harasses me after I file complaint?
**Illegal.** Document every call/message. File harassment complaint with IRDAI + police. We've seen this — it backfires on insurers.
