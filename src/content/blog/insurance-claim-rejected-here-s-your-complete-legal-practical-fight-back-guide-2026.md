## The 8 Most Common Rejection Reasons (And How to Beat Each)

### 1. Pre-Existing Disease (PED) Exclusion — #1 Reason (35% of rejections)

**Insurer says**: *"Condition existed before policy, excluded per waiting period."*

**Legal Reality**: 
- IRDAI defines PED narrowly (Circular IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/126/07/2016): **Condition for which treatment was taken or advised in 48 months before policy inception**
- **Controlled on medication without hospitalization for 48 months = NOT PED** (even if declared)
- Waiting period (typically 2–4 years) once completed = **covered**
- **Moratorium Rule (IRDAI 2023)**: After **8 continuous claim-free years**, insurers **cannot deny PED claims** on any ground

**Our Win Rate**: 70%+

**Counter-Argument Checklist:**
- [ ] Was condition declared in proposal form? (If yes → covered after waiting period)
- [ ] Has waiting period been completed? (Policy age > waiting period)
- [ ] Is condition "controlled without hospitalization for 48 months"? (IRDAI definition)
- [ ] Has policy completed 8 claim-free years? (Moratorium applies)
- [ ] Treating doctor certificate: current issue unrelated to declared condition?

**Template Argument for GRO:**
> "The rejected condition ([condition]) was declared in the proposal form dated [DATE]. The policy has been in force for [X] years, completing the [Y]-year waiting period on [DATE]. Per IRDAI Circular IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/126/07/2016, a condition controlled on medication without hospitalization for 48 months does not qualify as a pre-existing disease. The treating doctor's certificate (attached) confirms the current hospitalization is unrelated to the declared condition. Furthermore, the policy has completed [8+] continuous claim-free years, triggering the moratorium protection under IRDAI's 2023 amendment."

### 2. Non-Disclosure / Misrepresentation (Section 45) — 25% of rejections

**Insurer says**: *"You didn't disclose X, policy void under Section 45, Insurance Act 1938."*

**Legal Reality**:
- **Section 45**: After **3 years**, policy **cannot be questioned** except for **proven fraud**
- **Fraud ≠ Non-disclosure**. Fraud requires **intent to deceive** (Supreme Court: *LIC v. G.M. Channabasamma*, 2011)
- **Materiality test**: Would insurer have issued policy at same premium? If yes → not material
- **Insurer's underwriting acceptance**: Medical exam done + standard rates charged = accepted risk
- **Knowledge imputation**: Agent knew / medical exam revealed / hospital records available → insurer deemed to know

**Our Win Rate**: 65%+ (if policy >3 years, near 100%)

**Counter-Argument Checklist:**
- [ ] Policy age >3 years? (Section 45 bar applies — insurer must prove fraud)
- [ ] Was condition material? (Would premium/terms change?)
- [ ] Did insurer conduct medical exam? (Standard rates = accepted risk)
- [ ] Did agent/medical examiner know? (Knowledge imputed to insurer)
- [ ] Is non-disclosure innocent? (No intent to deceive = not fraud)

**Supreme Court Precedents to Cite:**
1. *LIC v. G.M. Channabasamma* (2011) — "Section 45 does not give insurer absolute power to repudiate; fraud must be proven beyond reasonable doubt"
2. *National Insurance Co. v. Seema Malhotra* (2013) — "Mere non-disclosure without fraudulent intent cannot avoid policy after 3 years"
3. *Kriti Singh v. Oriental Insurance* (2013) — "Insurer's own medical examination binds the insurer"

### 3. Treatment Not Covered / Experimental — 15% of rejections

**Insurer says**: *"Procedure not in policy / experimental / cosmetic / not medically necessary."*

**Legal Reality**:
- **Exclusions interpreted strictly against insurer** (*contra proferentem* principle)
- **"Experimental" misapplied** to standard-of-care treatments (new ≠ experimental)
- **IRDAI circular**: Network hospital denial of cashless ≠ claim denial
- **Treating doctor's opinion > insurer's medical advisor** (unless fraud)

**Our Win Rate**: 55%+

**Counter-Argument Checklist:**
- [ ] Is exclusion specific and unambiguous? (Vague exclusions fail in court)
- [ ] Is treatment standard of care per current medical guidelines? (Attach guidelines)
- [ ] Treating doctor's detailed justification on letterhead?
- [ ] Was cashless denied at network hospital? (IRDAI: insurer liable for network hospital denial)
- [ ] Is it a day-care procedure? (Most modern treatments are day-care — must be covered)

**Key Argument**: *"The exclusion clause is ambiguous and must be interpreted against the insurer (contra proferentem). The treatment is standard of care per [cite guideline — NCCN, ICMR, NICE, etc.]. The treating specialist's opinion (attached) carries more weight than the insurer's non-examining medical advisor."*

### 4. Cashless Pre-Authorization Denial — 12% of rejections

**Insurer says**: *"Pre-auth denied, so no cashless facility."*

**Legal Reality**:
- **Pre-auth denial ≠ claim rejection**. You can still claim **reimbursement**
- **Network hospitals are contractually bound** to provide cashless for covered treatments
- **IRDAI**: Network hospital denial of cashless = **deficiency in service** by insurer
- **Hospital cannot demand payment** if cashless should have been granted

**Our Win Rate**: 80%+

**Immediate Action Plan:**
1. Get denial in writing from hospital TPA desk (with reason code)
2. Ask hospital to re-submit with treating doctor's detailed justification
3. File reimbursement claim **simultaneously** — don't wait
4. Cite IRDAI circular: Network hospital denial = insurer's deficiency

### 5. Delay in Intimation / Document Submission — 8% of rejections

**Insurer says**: *"You informed us late / documents submitted late."*

**Legal Standard**: **"Reasonable time"** — not fixed days. Courts consider:
- Medical emergency / patient critical / ICU / unconscious
- Lack of awareness (first claim, elderly, rural)
- Insurer's own delays (TPA non-response, lost documents)
- Force majeure (floods, lockdowns, strikes, riots)

**Our Win Rate**: 75%+

**Valid Defenses with Evidence:**
| Defense | Evidence Needed |
|---------|-----------------|
| Medical emergency | ICU records, doctor's certificate, family affidavit |
| Lack of awareness | First claim affidavit, age/education proof, rural address |
| Insurer/TPA delay | Courier receipts, email trails, TPA acknowledgment |
| Documents lost by insurer | Submission proof, follow-up emails |
| Force majeure | News reports, government orders, local authority letters |

### 6. Fraud Allegation — 3% of rejections (but most dangerous)

**Insurer says**: *"Staged accident / fake documents / deliberate non-disclosure / fraud."*

**Legal Reality**:
- **Criminal standard of proof** required: *beyond reasonable doubt*
- Insurer must prove **intent to deceive** + **materiality** + **reliance**
- Most "fraud" allegations are **pressure tactics** to force low settlement
- **Burden of proof on insurer** — not on you to prove innocence

**Our Win Rate**: 40% (needs strong legal backing)

**If Accused of Fraud — Do This Immediately:**
1. **Do NOT sign anything** without lawyer review
2. **Hire a lawyer** specializing in insurance fraud defense (we refer)
3. **Preserve all originals** — do not hand over to insurer
4. **File counter-complaint** with IRDAI for harassment if allegations baseless
5. **Demand evidence** — insurer must produce proof of fraud

### 7. Policy Lapse / Premium Default — 2% of rejections

**Insurer says**: *"Policy lapsed, grace period over, no coverage."*

**Legal Reality**:
- **IRDAI mandates 30-day grace period** (15 days for monthly mode)
- **Revival options**: Ordinary revival (within 2 years), Special Revival (once per policy, shifts commencement date)
- **Lapsed policy ≠ void policy** — can be revived with interest
- **Death during grace period**: Claim payable (premium deducted from payout)

**Our Win Rate**: 85%+

**Revival Strategies:**
| Lapse Duration | Best Path | Documents |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| <6 months | Ordinary revival + back premiums + interest | DGH (Declaration of Good Health) |
| 6 months – 2 years | Ordinary revival (may need medical) | Medical exam if required |
| 2–5 years | **Special Revival Scheme** (once per policy) | DGH + back premiums + 9.5% compounded interest |
| >5 years | Surrender only (usually) | — |

### 8. Exclusion Clause Misapplication — Always Check

**Insurer says**: *"Exclusion X applies."*

**Legal Reality**:
- **Exclusions interpreted strictly against insurer** (*contra proferentem*)
- Must be **specific, unambiguous, and brought to policyholder's notice**
- **General exclusions** (war, nuclear, etc.) rarely apply to standard claims
- **Disease-specific exclusions** must match diagnosis exactly (ICD codes)

**Our Win Rate**: 60%+

## Insurer-Specific Tactics (What We've Learned)

### LIC (Life Insurance Corporation)
- **Strengths**: Process-driven, responds to regulation citations
- **Weakness**: Slow, bureaucratic, multiple handoffs
- **Tactic**: Cite specific IRDAI regulations by number. Escalate to Zonal Manager → Executive Director → Chairperson. Use RTI for claim file if needed.

### ICICI Lombard
- **Strengths**: Digital-first, fast GRO response
- **Weakness**: Over-relies on TPA medical opinions
- **Tactic**: Challenge TPA opinion with treating doctor's certificate. Escalate to Head of Claims directly.

### HDFC ERGO
- **Strengths**: Professional claims team, good network
- **Weakness**: Strict on room rent limits, sub-limits
- **Tactic**: Choose no-cap plans. If capped, argue proportional deduction is unfair per IRDAI guidelines.

### Star Health
- **Strengths**: Senior citizen specialist, Day 1 PED on Red Carpet
- **Weakness**: Strict on non-network hospitals
- **Tactic**: Use network hospitals. If emergency at non-network, cite "emergency at any registered hospital" clause.

### Care Health (formerly Religare)
- **Strengths**: Good senior plans, no co-pay on Senior
- **Weakness**: Cashless denials at own network hospitals
- **Tactic**: Cite IRDAI circular on network hospital obligation. Escalate to CEO office — they respond fast.

### Niva Bupa (formerly Max Bupa)
- **Strengths**: Strong restore, chronic care management
- **Weakness**: Documentation-heavy, slow reimbursement
- **Tactic**: Submit complete file upfront. Use chronic care program for pre-auth support.

### Bajaj Allianz
- **Strengths**: Motor claims expertise
- **Weakness**: Health claims — aggressive on "negligence" and IDV disputes
- **Tactic**: For motor: independent surveyor + FIR + security certificates. For health: challenge negligence with "reasonable care" standard.

### Public Sector (New India, Oriental, National, United)
- **Strengths**: Government-backed, honor Ombudsman awards
- **Weakness**: Slow, bureaucratic, TPA-dependent
- **Tactic**: IGMS complaint works very well. CMD office escalation effective.
