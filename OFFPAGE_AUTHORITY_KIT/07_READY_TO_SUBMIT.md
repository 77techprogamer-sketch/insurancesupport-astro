## 4. Quora — five finished answers

**Pacing: one answer per session.** Wait a day between. Answer in your own words — edit the drafts
below so they sound like you, then post. Sign every one:

> — Hari Kotian, IRDAI-Registered Insurance Advisor (Reg. 0149161D), Bengaluru

### Q1. What has your experience with the Insurance Ombudsman been?
https://www.quora.com/What-has-been-your-experience-with-the-insurance-Ombudsman-What-was-the-resolution-provided-and-how-long-did-they-take-before-reaching-this

> The Ombudsman is the most under-used remedy in Indian insurance, and it is free.
>
> Jurisdiction covers disputes up to ₹50 lakh per claim including expenses — the ceiling was raised
> from ₹30 lakh by the 2023 amendment to the Insurance Ombudsman Rules 2017. Most individual health,
> life and motor claims sit well under that, so for the majority of people this is the right forum,
> not a consumer court.
>
> Mechanics: file within one year of the insurer's written rejection (one month if the insurer never
> replied at all). The Ombudsman should issue an award within three months of receiving the
> complaint. No lawyer is needed and there is no fee. An award you accept binds the insurer — but it
> does not bind you, so you keep the consumer-court option if you are unhappy with it.
>
> The thing people get wrong is sequence. Go to the insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer first, and
> register on IRDAI's portal as well. Then the Ombudsman. Filing out of order is the most common
> reason a complaint is returned. Full filing sequence here: /blog/insurance-ombudsman-india-guide/

### Q2. How long does an insurance ombudsman take in India?
https://www.quora.com/How-long-does-an-insurance-ombudsman-take-for-a-case-assignment-and-hearing-in-India

> Statutorily the award should come within three months of the Ombudsman receiving the complaint.
> What actually determines whether you land near three months or near twelve is documentation.
>
> Three things slow cases down: filing without the insurer's written rejection letter, filing before
> the insurer's GRO has had a chance to respond, and a complaint that narrates events instead of
> tying each ground to a policy clause.
>
> What speeds it up: attach the repudiation letter, the policy schedule with the relied-upon clause
> highlighted, your GRO complaint and the insurer's reply, and a short note stating the exact relief
> you want in rupees. Complaints that arrive as a complete, indexed bundle get heard far faster.

### Q3. How do I file a case with the insurance ombudsman in India?
https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-file-a-case-with-insurance-ombudsman-in-India

> In this order:
>
> 1. **Insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer** — always first. Written complaint, keep the reference number.
> 2. **IRDAI** — register the grievance on the regulator's portal as well. This runs alongside the Ombudsman; it does not replace it and does not pause your deadline.
> 3. **Insurance Ombudsman** — within one year of the written rejection (one month if the insurer stayed silent). Ceiling ₹50 lakh per claim. Free, no lawyer, award within three months of receipt.
> 4. **Consumer Commission** — only above ₹50 lakh, or if you reject the Ombudsman's award.
>
> Pick the Ombudsman with jurisdiction over your area, not the insurer's head office. Complaints
> filed against the wrong office are the second most common reason for delay.
> Walkthrough: /blog/insurance-ombudsman-india-guide/

### Q4. Health insurance Medi-claim stuck 8 months — who should I hire?
https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-hire-a-specialist-or-expert-to-claim-a-health-insurance-Medi-Claim-It-has-been-stuck-for-8-months-now-and-I-m-ready-to-pay-also

> You don't need to hire anyone, and I would be cautious about paying for something the regulation
> gives you for free.
>
> Eight months of silence is not a rejection — it is a delay, and delay is separately actionable.
> Under IRDAI's 2024 policyholder regulations the insurer must decide within 30 days and raise any
> query within 15. Past that they owe penal interest.
>
> What to do this week: write to the insurer's GRO referring to the claim number and asking for a
> written decision within 15 days, citing the 30-day rule. In parallel register the grievance on
> IRDAI's portal. If nothing moves, the Ombudsman takes delay complaints as readily as rejection
> ones.
>
> The one thing genuinely worth paying a professional for is a case where the repudiation turns on
> medical necessity or a non-disclosure allegation — those need a properly evidenced rebuttal pack.
> A pure delay does not.
> Appeal sequence: /blog/how-to-appeal-rejected-insurance-claim-india-2026/

### Q5. Claim rejected — should I go to the District Consumer Commission?
https://www.quora.com/What-is-your-advice-for-someone-whose-insurance-claim-has-been-rejected-and-is-considering-filing-a-filing-a-complaint-with-the-District-Consumer-Commission

> Probably not first. The Ombudsman is free, needs no lawyer, must award within three months, and
> covers claims up to ₹50 lakh. The Consumer Commission is the right venue above that ceiling, or
> if you have already rejected an Ombudsman award.
>
> The asymmetry matters: an Ombudsman award binds the insurer but not you. Go there first and you
> keep the court route in reserve. Start at the Commission and you have spent time and money on the
> slower forum and given up the faster one.
>
> Before either, get the rejection in writing and match it to the clause they relied on. Most
> repudiations I see are defeated on documentation, not on legal argument.
