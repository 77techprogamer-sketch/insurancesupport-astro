Care Freedom: The Plan That Covers Diabetes and Hypertension in Year One — With Sub-Limits You Must Read First

Care Health's lifestyle-disease plan in a product case study: 2-year pre-existing wait instead of the usual three, 540+ day-care procedures, and the sub-limit trade-off at ₹3–5 lakh sum insured.

Product Case StudyCare HealthCare FreedomUpdated Oct 2026

Care Freedom was built for buyers who already have lifestyle conditions — diabetes, hypertension, cardiac history — and keep hitting the pre-existing disease wall. Its headline: a two-year pre-existing waiting period where the market standard is three. A 58-year-old policyholder with declared type-2 diabetes bought the plan precisely for that clause; the case below tracks how the clause, and the sub-limits that come with the cheaper plan options, shaped the first two claims.

The trade-off that decides this plan: the lower premium on the ₹3–5 lakh options comes attached to sub-limits on specific treatments. A cataract claim on Plan 1 is paid only up to the published cap, whatever the surgery costs. Buyers choosing Freedom for the 2-year PED clause often miss the sub-limit table — and meet it at the billing desk.
What worked for this policyholder: diabetes was declared at application and accepted at underwriting, so after the two-year PED wait, treatment for diabetic complications settled as a normal claim — no fresh waiting period, no co-payment shock. The annual health check-up also caught an HbA1c drift a year before it became a claim.

How we handled the two-year wait

  • Underwriting disclosure filed with full medication history — the acceptance letter became the basis for every later claim argument.
  • Renewal tracked on auto-debit with a manual date check; a lapsed renewal would restart the two-year wait.
  • Sub-limit table printed and taped inside the policy folder — the cataract cap was priced before the surgery was scheduled.
  • Claim documents assembled inside the 60/180-day pre/post hospitalisation windows.

FAQs

How long is the pre-existing wait in Care Freedom?Two years, against the three years that is standard in most Indian health plans — provided the condition was declared and accepted at underwriting.
Does Care Freedom have sub-limits?Yes on selected treatments under the ₹3–5 lakh Plan 1 options (cataract, knee replacement among them). The ₹7–10 lakh Plan 2 options carry the wider structure. Read the sub-limit table before choosing.
Can a family share one Care Freedom policy?Yes — floater cover for up to six members, with children covered to 24 years and lifelong renewability for adults.
Is premium taxable deduction available?Yes — Care Freedom premium qualifies under Section 80D of the Income Tax Act, 1961, subject to the applicable limits.
