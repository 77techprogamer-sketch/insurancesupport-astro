# The off-site plan

*Written 12 November 2026. This is the part that cannot be done from the codebase.*

## Why this file exists

Everything on-site is now in reasonable shape: 236 indexable pages, titles inside the SERP limit, no broken links, no invented statistics, 2.2 KB of JavaScript per page, sources cited on 67 articles, and a correct Google Business Profile. That gets a site into the running. It does not make it win.

Two signals decide the rest, and neither is a page:

1. **Who links to you.** A 4.3-star local business with 40 real review links and six local citations will outrank a better-written site with none.
2. **How many real reviews you have, and what they say.** For "insurance claim help Bengaluru", the map pack sits above the organic results. Map pack position is driven by proximity, category, review count and review recency — three of which are under your control today.

## Priority 1 — Google Business Profile cadence (weekly, 20 minutes)

The profile is `LIC - Hari Kotian`, 4.3 from 24 reviews. Three things to do on a schedule:

| Cadence | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Publish one post with **Call now** (copy in `GBP_OPTIMIZATION.md` §D) | Posts are a ranking input for the map pack and cost nothing |
| Weekly | Add one photo — office, desk, documents, the Jalahalli board | Profiles with fresh photos get materially more direction requests |
| Monthly | Answer the Q&A section yourself, with the questions people actually ask | Currently empty; anyone can post there, including competitors |
| Always | Reply to every new review within 48 hours, by name | Reply rate correlates with map pack position |

**The review gap that matters.** All 24 reviews are about LIC policy advice. Not one is about a rejected claim, which is what these 236 pages sell. Two or three reviews from claim clients would change what the Bengaluru and Karnataka pages can honestly claim, and it is the single highest-value thing available.

## Priority 3 — Links you can ask for by name (30 days)

Local links are cheap to earn and weigh heavily for "insurance claim help <city>":

1. **Apartment and residents' associations** in Jalahalli, Yelahanka, Hebbal, Mathikere, Vidyaranyapura. Offer a free 30-minute session on "what to do when a claim is rejected" and ask for a link from the events page. Ten associations is ten relevant local links.
2. **Hospitals' patient-education pages** — the ones that publish insurance help already. The ICICI Lombard cashless guide and the IRDAI pre-authorisation guide are the two to pitch.
3. **Motor dealers and garages** in Jalahalli and Peenya: a "claims help" partner listing on their site.
4. **Kannada and Bengaluru local media** — Deccan Herald's civic pages, Vijay Karnataka, Prajavani. Not a press release: a 500-word explainer on the Ombudsman deadline that a reporter can use as-is. One such placement is worth more than fifty directory listings.
5. **Consumer-advice and personal-finance sites** that cite regulators. Pitch the Section 45 explainer as a source, with the exact rule and citation already on the page.

### The pitch that works (120 words, adapt per recipient)

> I run an insurance advisory in Bengaluru and I keep a page on the Insurance Ombudsman route for policyholders whose claims were rejected — the 14-day grievance clock, the ₹50 lakh award limit, and the one-year filing window under the Ombudsman Rules, 2017. It is sourced to the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen and IRDAI's September 2024 master circular, and it covers the Karnataka side district by district.
>
> If you have a page where readers ask what to do after a rejection, it may be a useful link. No payment involved either way — I would rather it be cited because it is accurate. Happy to be corrected if anything in it is out of date.

## What not to do

- **No paid link networks, no PBNs, no "guest post packages".** The competitors in this niche do exactly that, and it is why their pages read like they were written by a link seller.
- **No comment spam** on news articles about claim rejections.
- **No review incentives** — Google's policy, and it also makes the reviews worthless to a reader who can smell it.
- **No fake "awarded by" badges.** An unearned trust badge is the same failure as an invented statistic.

## The honest expectation

Twelve to twenty genuinely relevant local links, plus 30 reviews with three of them about claims, would move this more than any further work on the pages. That work has a floor: the site is now as good as a site can be without someone vouching for it.
